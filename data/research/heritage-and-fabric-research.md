## Track 1 — Saudi heritage and culture

Almost every classic Saudi piece was built for one job first: heat, cold, privacy, or welcoming guests. The beauty came second. That is the "if you know, you know" key for A'ila Areeqa: copy the *reason*, not the costume.

### What people wore through the year

| Season | Men | Women | Why |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Summer | White cotton thobe; light, soft-yarn bisht for occasions | Light cotton or sheer overdresses (Najd) | White and cotton breathe and bounce back the sun |
| Autumn / spring | Same thobe; colors start to darken | Layered sheer overdress over a base dress | Desert nights get cool fast; layers come on and off |
| Winter | Brown or navy thobes, heavier wool; coarse camel-hair or goat-wool bisht; sheepskin farwa | Farwa or wool layers over the abaya | Northern and central nights can drop below freezing |

Sources: [Ithra on the thobe](https://www.ithra.com/en/news/saudi-thobe-ongoing-story), [Arab News on the bisht](https://arabnews.com/node/2014101), [Arab News on the farwa](https://arab.news/vb9wn).

### The bisht (men's cloak)

- **What it is:** a flat, rectangular cloak worn over the thobe. Classic colors run from white, beige and cream to brown, grey and black.
- **Made from:** camel hair and goat wool, spun and woven. Soft yarn for summer, coarse hair for winter. A 1949 bisht at the RISD Museum is plain-woven camel hair with metallic yarn trim. Gathering enough camel hair alone could take months.
- **Why:** it started as a Bedouin winter garment for cold desert nights. Over time it became the sign of rank. The gold braid was once kept for religious and political leaders.
- **Who makes it:** master tailors, many in Al-Ahsa. The Hasawi bisht is the most expensive kind. One Hofuf tailor's wife was also a bisht seamstress, skilled at collar embroidery. One bisht can take a week by hand.
- **Three main styles:** Darbeyah, Mekasar (also Gasbi, with silk embroidery on the edge), and Tarkeeb (tailored, with gold zari embroidery).
- **Design takeaway:** the bisht's power is the *edge*, not the body. A plain dark coat with a thin, hand-finished braid only on the front opening reads "bisht" to anyone who knows.

Sources: [RISD Museum bisht, 1949](https://risdmuseum.org/node/1212336), [Arab News](https://arabnews.pk/node/429954), [Malay Mail on Hofuf tailors](https://malaymail.com/news/life/2025/03/29/saudi-tailor-fights-to-save-ancient-bisht-craft-as-mass-produced-robes-dominate-market/171203).

### The thobe, shemagh and agal

- **Thobe:** the white cotton thobe became the national everyday garment after unification under King Abdulaziz. Cotton was picked because it breathes. In winter, men switch to brown and navy and heavier wool.
- **Shemagh:** the red-and-white checked head cloth, held by the **agal**, a cord traditionally made of sheep wool. *Speculation flag:* the story that the shemagh goes back to ancient Sumer is a legend, not proven history.
- **Design takeaway:** the checked grid of the shemagh can live as a tiny jacquard (woven-in) pattern on a pocket lining or a cuff, in tone-on-tone color, so only a Saudi eye catches it.

Source: [Ithra](https://www.ithra.com/en/news/saudi-thobe-ongoing-story).

### The farwa (winter coat)

- **What it is:** a long, loose coat lined with sheepskin or fur. The cut is close to the bisht. Traditional ones are plain black or brown.
- **Where and why:** mostly the north and center, where towns like Turaif average as low as −5°C in winter. People wear it at home and on desert camping trips (kashta).
- **Today:** women now wear farwas in place of an abaya in winter. Saudi brand Abadia put one on Queen Rania.
- **Design takeaway:** this is your easiest bridge garment for men and women. Same cut, different weight and trim.

Sources: [Arab News](https://arabnews.pk/node/1776911), [Harper's Bazaar Arabia](https://www.harpersbazaararabia.com/fashion/the-news/queen-rania-wears-okhtein-abadia-daughters-graduation), [Asharq Al-Awsat](https://en-archive.aawsat.com/2014/01/01/saudis-suit-up-for-winter/).

### Women's dress by region

- **Najd (center):** loose shapes, ankle- or floor-length, with embroidery. Najdi women first wore muted grey and navy; brighter colors came later. Many sewed and cut their own clothes.
- **The Najdi overdress (thawb najdi):** a huge T-shaped dress of sheer tulle with wide sleeves and big underarm panels, embroidered at the neck front and back. Locally it is called *munaykhal*, "mesh strainer," because the fabric is full of holes. Worn for special occasions.
- **Hijaz (west):** shows outside influence from trade and pilgrimage. Ceremonial dress is white with gold zari thread.
- **Asir and Jazan (south):** geometric, many-colored robes, vivid purples, and floral headpieces. Asiri women wore straw hats because they worked outdoors.
- **Design takeaway (feminine → masculine):** the *munaykhal* idea — a sheer outer layer over a solid one — works for men too. Think a dark, open-weave summer overshirt over a white thobe or tee. Same idea, sharper shoulders, no embroidery.

Sources: [Arab News on Najdi fashion](https://www.arabnews.com/node/2381461/amp), [Zay Initiative object note](https://thezay.org/product/zi1990-50071-ksaembroidered-overgarment-with-zari-saudi-arabia), [Google Doodle note, Saudi National Day 2017](https://doodles.google/doodle/saudi-arabia-national-day-2017/), [Arab News on regional gowns](https://www.arabnews.com/node/216382).

### Al Sadu weaving

- **What it is:** Bedouin weaving done on a ground loom. The name means "weaving done in a horizontal style." It is a tight, tough, warp-faced plain weave. UNESCO listed it for Saudi Arabia and Kuwait in 2020.
- **Who made it:** mostly older Bedouin women, master weavers who teach inside the household. Saudipedia notes men also wove, but the detailed patterns are credited to women.
- **Made from:** sheep wool (the finest), goat hair and camel hair. Used for tents, cushions, sacks, rugs, camel and horse gear — and bisht cloth.
- **Why it looks the way it does:** the shapes copy the desert: dunes, grass, palms, camels. Bright reds and oranges were added to liven up a plain landscape.
- **Named patterns:** Shajarah (the "tree," the hardest one, a central strip of small squares each showing a piece of Bedouin life), Uwairjan, and Asnan al-Khail ("horse teeth").
- **Design takeaway:** Sadu is now everywhere in tourist goods, so a loud Sadu print will read cheap. Use one Sadu motif, shrunk, woven into a waistband, collar stand, or bag strap, in two quiet colors.

Sources: [UNESCO](https://ich.unesco.org/en/RL/traditional-weaving-of-al-sadu-01586), [Saudipedia](https://saudipedia.com/en/what-is-al-sadu), [AramcoWorld](https://www.aramcoworld.com/articles/2025/ja25/al-sadu-weaves-stories-of-culture-and-identity), [Abu Dhabi Culture](https://abudhabiculture.ae/en/heritage-records/intangible-cultural-heritage/al-sadu-unesco), [248am on Shajarah](https://248am.com/?p=581198).

### Al-Qatt Al-Asiri (women's wall painting)

- **What it is:** painted geometric walls inside homes in Asir, on white gypsum. UNESCO listed it in 2017.
- **Who and why:** women painted it, mainly in the guest room, because that room shows a family's pride. Neighbors competed. Women gathered gypsum from mountains and caves. A famous master was Fatima Abou Gahas, who died in 2010.
- **The pattern language:** triangles stand for family. Bottom stripes are called "palms of the hands"; women measured them with their fingers. Vertical lines between triangles are called "combs." Colors are red, yellow, blue and black.
- **Design takeaway (feminine → masculine):** keep the *structure* and drop the colors. Stacked triangles and finger-width bands in black and sand on a men's knit or a scarf border feel bold, not decorative.

Sources: [UNESCO](https://ich.unesco.org/en/RL/01261), [UNESCO inventory PDF](https://ich.unesco.org/doc/src/34853.pdf), [Ithra](https://ithraeyat.ithra.com/editions/threads/art-by-women-for-all-al-qatt-al-asiri), [Architizer](https://architizer.com/blog/?p=188528).

### Najdi houses (center)

- **What they are:** thick mud-brick walls, inward-facing courtyards, narrow shaded streets. Diriyah, the birthplace of the first Saudi state, is the best example.
- **The triangles:** small triangle and square holes in the walls (*furjat*) let air and light in and let people see out. They were also used for defense. They are stacked into patterns on the walls.
- **The guest room:** the *qahwa* (majlis) got the most decoration, to show generosity. A slot in the ceiling let smoke from the coffee fire out.
- **Design takeaway:** a row of small laser-cut or stitched triangle vents under the arm or across a yoke. It does the same job — air flow — and tells the Najd story.

Sources: [Wikipedia: Najdi architecture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Najdi_architecture), [Saudipedia: al-Jawf architecture](https://saudipedia.com/en/article/3155/culture/traditional-architecture-in-al-jawf-province).

### Hijazi rawasheen (west)

- **What they are:** carved wooden boxes that stick out from house fronts in Jeddah, Makkah, Madinah, Yanbu and Taif.
- **Why:** shade, airflow and privacy. You could see out; no one could see in. They also showed wealth.
- **Details:** the wood was bought from India, Indonesia and Sudan. Classic colors are natural wood, browns and green. One researcher describes them as a "garment" wrapped around the building.
- **Already used in fashion:** designer Makram Marzuki turned a rawasheen pattern from Beit Nassif into a bridal look. So the obvious version is taken.
- **Design takeaway:** the subtle version is the *idea* of see-out-not-in. Example: a fine lattice-pattern lining that only shows when a jacket opens.

Sources: [Saudipedia](https://saudipedia.com/en/article/530/culture/cultural-affairs/al-rawashin), [Arab News on Makkah](https://arabnews.com/node/1847861), [WIT Press paper](https://www.witpress.com/elibrary/wit-transactions-on-ecology-and-the-environment/128/20814), [Arab News on Banat Al-Balad](https://arab.news/b63v2).

### Coffee, the dallah and the majlis

- **What it is:** Saudi coffee is lightly roasted and boiled with cardamom, saffron or clove. It is poured from a long-spouted *dallah* into a small handleless cup (*finjal*), served with dates.
- **The rules:** small pours so guests get refills. Guests shake the cup to say "enough." UNESCO listed Arabic coffee and the majlis in 2015.
- **Design takeaway:** the dallah's curved spout and the finjal's tiny size are strong shapes for hardware — a zip pull, a button, a pendant.

Sources: [Mitsubishi Corp. "Local Lens"](https://www.mitsubishicorp.com/jp/en/ryowa/series/local-lens/261003/), [Michelin Guide](https://guide.michelin.com/en/food-and-travel-saudi-arabia), [Malay Mail](https://www.malaymail.com/news/life/2022/10/08/taste-of-qatari-hospitality-traditional-arabic-coffee/32408).

### Natural dyes (where the colors came from)

- **Madder root** for red, **dried limes** for green, **pomegranate skins** for purple, **turmeric** for yellow — as documented by Joy Totah Hilden in *Bedouin Weaving of Saudi Arabia and Its Neighbors*.
- **Indigo** for blue. Indigo dyeing was a big trade in Yemen, especially Zabid.
- **Design takeaway:** a palette built only from these plant colors (madder red, lime green, indigo, turmeric) is heritage on its own — no pattern needed.

Sources: [Al Jadid on Hilden's book](https://aljadid.com/node/1557), [Al-Bab review of *Indigo in the Arab World*](https://al-bab.com/book-review-indigo-arab-world).

#### Najdi and Bedouin men's winter layers

- **What it is:** In cold weather, men added layers over the white *thobe*. Ithra names the *sedairi* (*sudairi*), a warm vest of broadcloth, and the *jebba*, a long robe worn over a shirt. In winter men chose brown and navy, and heavier cloth like wool.
- **The outer robes:** The *zaboon* is a long robe to the ankles, sleeves to the wrist. It is open at the front, with buttons at the top and small side slits. The *dagla* is worn over the thobe, often decorated and of heavier cloth, for warmth or big days.
- **Rich men's winter wear:** In Najd, the *jokh* was costly winter cloth that showed wealth. The *labda* was a local wool garment padded with cotton so the wool would not scratch the skin. The *barqa* was a two-color woven winter cloak worn by princes and sheikhs.
- **The *sirwal* (under-trousers):** Long, roomy pants with a drawstring, worn under the thobe. No good source says what Najdi sirwal was made of. Speculation: plain cotton, because it breathes and was cheap.
- **Design takeaway:** Hide a padded cotton lining inside a wool overshirt or vest, like the *labda*. Only the wearer feels it. Give a men's vest the *sedairi* look: broadcloth in winter brown or navy. For women, the same vest can sit long and open over an abaya.

Sources: [Ithra – The Saudi Thobe](https://www.ithra.com/en/news/saudi-thobe-ongoing-story), [Saudipedia – Ardah costumes](https://saudipedia.com/en/what-are-the-traditional-costumes-for-the-saudi-ardah), [Saudipedia – Eastern Province attire](https://saudipedia.com/en/list-of-traditional-attire-of-the-eastern-province), [Asharq Al-Awsat – Saudi traditional dress](https://english.aawsat.com/home/article/3489611/saudi-traditional-dress-remains-fashionable-kingdoms-founding-3-centuries-ago), [Saudi Arabesque – urban men's dress](https://saudiarabesque.com/traditional-urban-men-s-dress-of-saudi-arabia/).

#### Women's silver jewelry (Najran, the south, Bedouin)

- **What it is:** Najran pieces include *kharous* (earrings), *harouz* (necklaces), *halqah* (head piece), *lazem* and *shamiliyyat* (wide bracelets) and *marda'* (rings). Older necklaces were amber and branch-shaped beads.
- **Materials:** Southern jewelry was mostly big silver with few set stones, and silver wire and plates on top. It used fire agate from Yemen, amber, blood coral and old glass beads. *Ra'af* means deep red coral beads. Bedouin silver also used carnelian, turquoise, bells and coins. Some bracelets weighed about 200 g.
- **Why it was worn:** Part of the bride price bought her jewelry. It was her own property and safety net, sold in hard times. Some necklace cases held Qur'an verses, and turquoise was worn against the evil eye.
- **Sound and movement:** Tiny bells told people a woman was near, so men looked away as she passed. She was "heard but not seen."
- **Who made it:** In Yemen many top silversmiths were Jewish; most left around 1948–1952. In Saudi Arabia, smiths worked in Riyadh's Batha souq and in Taif, remaking old pieces in old styles. Speculation: Najran pieces came from Yemeni or Jewish smiths; no source says this for Najran itself.
- **Design takeaway:** For men, one small silver bead or a closed "bell" with no clapper (silent) on a drawstring end or zip pull. Or one deep coral-red stitch inside a cuff, a nod to *ra'af*. A woman's piece becomes a quiet men's detail.

Sources: [Riyadh Daily – Najran women's jewelry](https://alriyadhdaily.com/article/762e67d9dd6b43e1b57ececa0241e89e), [Saudipedia – Jewelry Crafting](https://saudipedia.com/en/jewelry-crafting-in-saudi-arabia), [UNESCO – Bedouin jewellery](https://en.unesco.org/silkroad/content/traditional-bedouin-crafts-jewellery), [Arab News – Hidden Treasures](https://www.arabnews.com/art-culture/hidden-treasures-the-jewelry-of-saudi-arabia-goes-on-display-in-dubai-1475196), [AramcoWorld 1979](https://archive.aramcoworld.com/issue/197902/the.beauty.of.bedouin.jewelry.htm), [AramcoWorld 1973](https://archive.aramcoworld.com/issue/197302/bangles.from.the.sands.htm), [AramcoWorld 2012 – Yemeni Silver](https://archive.aramcoworld.com/issue/201201/silver.the.enduring.craft.of.yemeni.silver.htm).

#### The janbiya and southern leatherwork

- **What it is:** The *janbiya* is a curved dagger worn at the front of a belt. In Najran it is iron or steel, with a horn handle, often with silver or gold added. The best sheaths are inlaid with silver. Rich men's belts were woven with gold and silver thread.
- **Where and why:** It stands for courage, manhood and tribe. When tribes make peace, they lay daggers down. Many boys get one in their teens. In the Jazan mountains, men wear it at the middle of a leather belt, with flower-and-herb crowns.
- **Who made it:** Najran's Al-Janabi souq is known for janbiya makers. Prices run from SR 20–50 up to SR 250,000 or more. Najran leather workers also make the *masbat* (waist belt), *mezab* (baby carrier) and *zamalah* (big storage case).
- **Other leather:** Hides were camel, cattle, sheep and goat. Plant tanning gave a light red (*Calligonum*) or deep red (St John's wort). Pieces include the *nas'ah* (men's belt) and the *haskal*, a money pouch worn under the left arm on a braided strap. Sandals had camel-skin soles and goat-skin tops.
- **Design takeaway:** A belt or bag strap in plant-tanned leather, soft *Calligonum* red, with one small engraved silver piece. Or a slim inside pocket under the left arm, cut like the *haskal*. For women: a slim waist belt with the janbiya's center point as a flat silver plate.

Sources: [Arab News – Why the janbiya remains a symbol](https://www.arabnews.com/node/2059516/amp), [Arab News – Najran's janbiya](https://www.arabnews.com/saudi-arabia/najrans-janbiya-a-timeless-symbol-of-cultural-heritage-2595464), [Saudipedia – Najran handicrafts](https://saudipedia.com/en/list-of-handicrafts-in-najran-province), [Arab News – Najran leather artisans](https://www.arabnews.pk/saudi-arabia/najrans-leather-artisans-preserve-cultural-heritage-2566716), [Saudipedia – Leather Goods](https://saudipedia.com/en/leather-goods-in-the-kingdom), [CS Monitor – Daisies and daggers](https://www.csmonitor.com/World/Middle-East/2022/0613/Daisies-and-daggers-In-Saudi-mountains-garlands-crown-the-brave).

#### Incense, oud and Taif rose

- **What it is:** The *mabkhara* is a charcoal incense burner: a square base with sloping sides and a square metal-lined cup on top. Old ones were clay, metal or carved wood, some with mirrors. Amber and oud wood are burned on hot coals.
- **How clothes were scented:** The smoke scents the room, clothes, hair and guests. At weddings the burner is passed round so each guest can scent himself. Speculation: the *ghutra* was held over the smoke too; no source names it.
- **Taif rose water:** Over 910 farms in Taif grow about 550 million roses a year. The bush blooms once a year for 40–50 days; roses are picked early each morning. *Al-Arus* ("the bride's") rose water is about 80% strength, *al-thanou* about 50%, *al-sayer* 20% or less.
- **Today:** Saudi Arabia imported about 1.1 million kg of oud wood in nine months (2023–24). No source found on storing scent in clothing chests. Speculation: clothes were kept with incense or in scented chests.
- **Design takeaway:** Wrap each garment in tissue lightly scented with oud *bakhoor*. Or sew a tiny pocket inside the collar for a scent card. *Al-Arus* could be a hidden name on a label.

Sources: [Saudipedia – The Incense Burner](https://saudipedia.com/en/the-incense-burner), [Ithraeyat – The Art of Hospitality](https://ithraeyat.ithra.com/editions/identity/the-art-of-hospitality), [Arab News – 6 perfume traditions](https://www.arabnews.com/the-six/6-perfume-traditions-from-the-region-2591135), [Arab News – Taif's roses](https://www.arabnews.pk/saudi-arabia/blooming-once-a-year-taifs-roses-embody-regions-heritage-2458111), [Saudipedia – Taif rose water](https://saudipedia.com/en/how-is-taif-rose-water-made), [Gulf News – Oud sales](https://gulfnews.com/world/gulf/saudi/scent-of-eid-incense-and-oud-sales-soar-in-saudi-arabia-1.102051066).

#### Eastern Province pearl divers

- **What it is:** On the Eastern Province coast, the main dive season lasted 130 days. Divers dove about 2 minutes at a time, down to 18 m, about 50 dives a day.
- **What divers wore:** These sources cover the whole Gulf. Divers wore *sirwal* shorts and the *shamshool*, usually black. Others describe a white cotton suit or cotton overalls against jellyfish. The *fattam* was a bone or horn nose clip. The *khabt* were leather finger covers against sharp rocks. Beeswax or wool went in the ears.
- **Who did what:** On a Saudi boat the *nukhdah* (captain) took 20% of the catch, the diver 40%, the rope-puller 30% and the helper 10%. The *tawwash* (pearl merchant) went boat to boat to buy. Buyer and seller agreed a price by touching fingers under a red cloth.
- **Merchants' dress:** No source found on what the *tawawish* wore. Speculation: a bisht and fine thobe. A diver who found a rich oyster bed earned new clothes from the captain.
- **Design takeaway:** Use the *khabt* finger cover as the idea for a small leather tab on a cuff or glove. Or a red silk lining that only the wearer sees, a nod to the red cloth of secret deals. A matte black cotton piece can nod to the *shamshool*.

Sources: [Saudipedia – Diving in Saudi Arabia](https://saudipedia.com/en/article/3630/society/sports/diving-in-saudi-arabia), [Saudipedia – Historical Maritime Occupations](https://saudipedia.com/en/historical-maritime-occupations-in-saudi-arabia), [Abu Dhabi Culture – Diving equipment](https://abudhabiculture.ae/en/cultural-heritage/intangible/heritage-register/traditional-handicraft/diving-equipment-in-the-past), [Saudi British Society – Diving for Pearls](https://saudibritishsociety.org.uk/news-and-events/agm-lecture-diving-for-pearls-by-ms-julia-johnson/), [AramcoWorld – Servants of the Pearl](https://archive.aramcoworld.com/issue/199005/servants.of.the.pearl.htm), [Qatar Digital Library – Pearl Diving](https://www.qdl.qa/divers-are-pearl’s-best-friend-pearl-diving-gulf-1840s–1930s).

#### The agal

- **What it is:** The *agal* is the black ring worn on top of the *ghutra* or *shemagh* to hold it in place. Saudipedia says the name may come from the rope used to tie a camel's legs. This is a common story, but not proven.
- **How it is made:** The best ones use black goat hair (*al-mar'az*), because it is deep black and lasts. Others use wool, silk, cotton or nylon. A white cotton core is covered in black cotton, then wrapped in black thread on a 2 m wooden frame (*dazqa*). The ends are knotted, stitched, then shaped with a mold and mallet.
- **Old and royal types:** The *muqassab* (also *shatfah*) has ten sides and is wrapped in gold or silver thread. Kings Abdulaziz, Saud and Faisal wore it, so it is also called the "Faisal agal". A white agal came before the black one and is now rare. Older agals were thicker, some with two cords hanging at the back.
- **Who made it:** Taif is known for fine agals. In Makkah, the Abu Rish and Al-Hariri families are named for the craft. In Riyadh, the Al-Dirah market near Al-Hukm Palace still makes them.
- **Note:** Two Saudipedia pages differ. One says the *muqassab* is gold-wrapped. The other says camel hair, light brown or white. Check before using in brand copy.
- **Design takeaway:** Borrow the wrapped segments of the *muqassab*. Make a black-on-black, thread-wrapped band at a collar stand or cuff, with no gold. For women, the same wrapped cord becomes a thin belt or neckline binding. It reads Saudi without looking like menswear.

Sources: [Saudipedia – Al-Egal craftsmanship](https://saudipedia.com/en/al-egal-craftsmanship-in-saudi-arabia), [Saudipedia – Al-Egal](https://saudipedia.com/en/al-egal), [FIT Fashion History – Agal](https://fashionhistory.fitnyc.edu/agal/).

#### The barqa cloak and Al-Ahsa bisht weaving

- **What it is:** The *Hasawi bisht* is named after Al-Ahsa, where its weaving and gold trim were perfected. Arab News says Al-Ahsa has had the best bisht tailors for over 200 years. The *bisht al-barqa* is a goat-wool version made against cold desert nights. Imam Abdullah bin Saud (1814–1818) made it famous, and it is linked to Diriyah.
- **How it is made:** The main yarns are camel hair and sheep wool. Fine yarn also came from Iraq, Iran, India and the Levant. Saudipedia lists eight stages. In the last one, *al-bardakh*, the gold thread is beaten to bring out its shine. A handmade Hasawi takes 80–120 hours, with four tailors each doing one job.
- **Trim and colors:** *Zari* is silk or cotton yarn coated in real gold or silver. Arab News names three styles: *darbeyah* (square, loose, real zari), *mekasar* or *gasbi* (silk stitching along the edge), and *tarkeeb* (fitted, with gold zari). Old colors are white, beige, cream, brown, grey and black. Black with gold is the most popular. No source was found on woven stripe patterns.
- **Who makes it, and its status:** Arab News names the Al-Qattan, Al-Kharas, Al-Mahdi and Al-Bagli families. In 2025, UNESCO listed "Bisht (men's Abaa): skills and practices" as shared heritage of nine states, including Saudi Arabia. A Hofuf tailor says cheap machine-made robes are hurting the hand craft.
- **Design takeaway:** Use a *mekasar*-style edge: one thin line of tone-on-tone silk stitching along a jacket front, in place of gold. Make a goat-wool "barqa" overcoat in undyed cream or brown for winter. For women, put the same edge line on a long open coat, worn draped like a bisht.

#### Flower and herb crowns of Asir and Jazan

- **What it is:** The "flower men" are tribal men of the Jazan and Asir mountains. They wear crowns of fresh flowers and herbs. Ithra calls the crown *'asaib al-tayyib*. Saudipedia gives local names: *al-gharas* or *al-lawiyyah* in the Sarawat mountains, *al-mikhdhara* in Tihamah. Flowers at the center front are *al-mankousa*.
- **Which plants:** Sources name wild basil, marigold, fenugreek, white jasmine, wormwood, rue, yarrow, marjoram, lavender, daisies and more. Jasmine (*al-ful*) is for the finest crowns and lasts about two days. Screwpine flowers are used too, called *al-shalfa* in Tihamah. Speculation: the "*kadi*" plant is probably this screwpine, but no source used that name.
- **Why they wore them:** Mainly for scent. CS Monitor calls it "wearable aromatherapy", and wearers say it calms them. Al Jazeera says people believe it cures headaches. It also shows beauty, local pride and bravery, worn with the *janbiya*. Simple crowns are for every day; fancy ones for Ramadan and weddings. No source said it was for cooling.
- **Design takeaway:** Hide a tiny basil-and-jasmine scent sachet in a collar stand or hat band. Or embroider a small tonal sprig at the center back neck, like the *mankousa*. To keep it masculine, use herb leaves (basil, rue) instead of blooms, green on green. Men wearing scent is the southern idea; it never has to look floral.

Sources: [Saudipedia – Personal ornamental plants](https://saudipedia.com/en/personal-ornamental-plants), [Al Jazeera – The flower men of Saudi Arabia](https://www.aljazeera.com/gallery/2019/3/12/the-flower-men-of-saudi-arabia/), [CS Monitor – Daisies and daggers](https://proof.csmonitor.com/World/Middle-East/2022/0613/Daisies-and-daggers-In-Saudi-mountains-garlands-crown-the-brave), [Ithraeyat – Flower men from the south](https://ithraeyat.ithra.com/articles/flower-men-from-the-south).

#### Keeping scent in clothes at home

- **What it is:** Saudipedia says "no Saudi home is without" the charcoal incense burner (*mabkhara*). Oud and amber burn on hot charcoal. The smoke scents the room, clothes, bodies and guests.
- **Dukhoon kept between clothes:** In Abha, women make *dukhoon* from oud, musk, amber and rose oils, sometimes kneaded with boiled dates. They press it into small round discs and dry them for about a week. The discs are kept between clothes so they stay fresh-smelling.
- **Layering:** A Smithsonian piece describes letting incense smoke drift around clothes, then adding perfume so it lasts for days. Some people add oils as a third or fourth layer.
- **Not found:** No source was found for a wicker frame over the burner for clothes. The only *mandoos* chest source was Omani, and it did not mention scent. No source described oud oil put straight on cloth.
- **Design takeaway:** Sew a small loose-weave pocket inside a hem or lining, sized to hold a *dukhoon* disc. The garment keeps scent the old way, and only the wearer knows. Ship coats in a cedar-lined, lightly scented box. Speculation: whether Saudi chests were scented is not confirmed.

Sources: [Saudipedia – The incense burner](https://saudipedia.com/en/the-incense-burner), [Arab News – Saudi women learn the secrets of incense making](https://www.arabnews.com/news/572161), [Arab News – 6 perfume traditions](https://www.arabnews.com/the-six/6-perfume-traditions-from-the-region-2591135), [Smithsonian Folklife – Scents and memory in Arab perfume](https://festival.si.edu/blog/scents-and-memory-in-arab-perfume), [Encyclopedia of Crafts – Mandoos](https://encyclocraftsapr.com/mandoos/).

#### Najran mud towers

- **What it is:** Najran has mud houses in more than 40 villages, among palms and farms. The most common style is *al-darb*, a tower of about seven floors. Other styles are *al-mushulaq* (2–3 floors, U or L shape) and *al-qasbah* (round, wide at the base, narrow at the top, like a guard tower).
- **How it is built:** Builders start with a row of stones (*al-madmak*). Mud walls rise in layers on top. Each layer must dry before the next: one day in summer, two to three in winter. Roofs use palm trunks or tamarisk wood, coated in mud and lime. Al-Aan Palace (1688) is all mud on a stone base.
- **Why tall, and the white trim:** No source clearly says why they are tall. Clues: in Al-Aan Palace each floor housed one family, and round towers sat on corners for defense. Doors and windows are framed in white gypsum. White bands near the top are for looks. Speculation: tall houses may have saved farmland.
- **Design takeaway:** Use "white frame on earth": thin chalk-white piping around a pocket or placket on a mud-brown or sand garment, like gypsum around a Najran window. Or stack fine tonal stitch lines up the back of an overshirt, like drying mud layers. Works for men and women alike.
- **Still not found:** No source says what Najdi *sirwal* was made of. No source says what pearl merchants (*tawawish*) wore; a Kuwait Times piece only lists what they carried: scales, magnifiers and a red cloth toolkit called a *dasta*.

Sources: [Arab News – Najran's architectural legacy](https://www.arabnews.com/node/2587995/saudi-arabia), [Arab News – Mud houses in Najran](https://arabnews.com/node/1967321/saudi-arabia), [Arab News – Najran restores mud houses](https://www.arabnews.com/node/2258486/), [Arab News – Al-Aan Palace](https://www.arabnews.com/saudi-arabia/the-place-al-aan-palace-in-najran-an-architectural-gem-in-saudi-arabia-2182281), [Arab News – Al-Mishkat's mud palaces](https://www.arabnews.pk/node/2638865/saudi-arabia), [Kuwait Times – Pearl traders](https://kuwaittimes.com/pearl-traders-pillar-kuwaits-economy).

#### AlUla Old Town and northern dress

- **What it is:** AlUla Old Town sits on the old Hajj road between the Levant and Makkah. It has about 900 mud-brick houses, five courtyards and 400 old shops. Travellers wrote about it from the 1100s to the 1900s.
- **How it was built:** Houses stand in tight clusters for defence. Narrow, winding mud lanes link them. Rooftops shade the lanes. Fourteen gates opened each morning and shut each evening.
- **Men in the north:** In Hail, settled men wore a wide white *thobe* with wrist-length sleeves, for easy movement. They added a *sidariya* (a sleeveless vest lined with cotton or wool) for warmth or show. Bedouin men wore the wide-sleeved *thobe mrodon*, a red cotton *shal* on the head, and a short, tough *bidi* cloak they also slept in. In Al-Jouf, men wore the *mrodon* with a long embroidered *daqla* coat, or the *saya*, a light white robe with very faint embroidery.
- **Women in the north:** Al-Jouf women wore the *mahwathal*, a long dress folded at the waist and held by a woven belt. Brides wore the *murshida*, a fine wool cloak stitched with gold *zari*. In Tabuk, women wore a *sidariya* with gold buttons over a cotton *sirwal*, solid black or with dark stripes. Older women wore light colours; young women wore bright ones.
- **Why:** Saudipedia says these clothes first kept out the weather. Later they also showed a person's place and wealth. In AlUla today, women at Madrasat Addeera hand-stitch cloth dyed with colours from local rocks and plants.
- **Design takeaway:** Hide very faint tone-on-tone stitching on a white robe, like the Al-Jouf *saya*. To make the women's gold-button *sidariya* masculine, keep the cut but use bone or dull-silver buttons in the same colour as the cloth.

#### The sidari and the mrodon (Hijaz and the north)

- **About the sirwal:** Still no source found for the men's Najdi *sirwal* or old men's undergarments. Every *sirwal* page found today was about women's dress.
- **What the sidari is:** A vest worn over the *thobe*. In Makkah it came with or without sleeves. The sleeveless kind is the *maytan*, with cord trim (*qaytan*) at the neck and pockets. In Madinah, merchants and notables wore costly ones with silver buttons.
- **What the mrodon is:** A long *thobe* with very wide sleeves (*ardan*). In Madinah villages it had no buttons. In Makkah's desert villages it was tied with a leather belt. Saudipedia says King Abdulaziz's fighters first wore the Al-Jouf version, and royal court staff still wear it.
- **Other Hijazi menswear:** The Madinah *imama* (turban) was fine palm-frond strips covered in coloured silk, lined with white or blue silk. Men sometimes laid a *shal* over one shoulder. The Madinah *thobe* had pockets for money and a *subha* (prayer beads).
- **Design takeaway:** Use the *maytan*'s cord trim as one thin, self-colour piping line at a collar or pocket edge. Or hint at the *mrodon*: a slightly fuller hidden sleeve gusset (an extra panel under the arm), not a dramatic shape.

#### The tawawish (pearl merchants)

- **Their dress:** No source found for what *tawawish* wore. Three pages were checked.
- **What they did:** The *tawash* sailed out to the diving boats about a month after the divers left. He checked the pearls and bargained for them. He brought food to the boats and carried messages to the divers' families. Then he sold the pearls in Bahrain and India.
- **Status:** Al-Madina calls him the head of the team on his ship. He chose when the trip began and ended. Kuwait Times says merchants lived and ate better than divers, with bigger boats. One big pearl (*dana*) could make a merchant rich.
- **Tools:** The *tous* were round copper bowls with holes of different sizes, for sorting pearls. The *mizan* was a very fine scale kept in a copper box. Weights were copper or *aqeeq* (agate). Everything went in a *dasta*, a toolkit usually red and about one metre long. Some merchants later settled in Bombay; one, Hajji Muhammad Ali Zainal, came from Jeddah.
- **Design takeaway:** Speculation: the pierced *tous* bowls could inspire a tiny row of punched holes on a leather tab. Or line an inner pocket in soft red, a quiet nod to the red *dasta*.

Sources: [Al-Madina – The tawawish](https://www.al-madina.com/article/153107/%D8%A7%D9%84%D8%B7%D9%88%D8%A7%D8%B4%D9%88%D9%86-%D9%85%D8%B7%D8%A7%D8%B1%D8%AF%D9%88-%D8%A7%D9%84%D9%84%D8%A2%D9%84%D8%A6-%D9%88%D8%AF%D8%A7%D8%B9%D9%85%D9%88-%D8%A7%D9%84%D8%B5%D9%8A%D8%A7%D8%AF%D9%8A%D9%86), [Kuwait Times – Pearl traders](https://kuwaittimes.com/pearl-traders-pillar-kuwaits-economy), [PSAS – Pearl merchants in Bombay](https://www.archaeopresspublishing.com/ojs/index.php/PSAS/article/view/1212).

#### The muqassab agal: gold thread or camel hair?

- **What it is:** A stiff, square agal, also called *shatfa* or the "kings' agal". King Abdulaziz and his sons Saud and Faisal wore it. Makkah was known for it, and the Al-Hariri family there for its quality. It is rarely worn today.
- **The conflict:** Saudipedia's "Agal" page says camel hair, light brown or white, with two hanging threads. Saudipedia's "Agal making" page says two thin rolls fully wrapped in gold-coloured *zari* (metal thread). Al-Watan says it is gold-coloured and square, mostly gold or silver thread.
- **Best answer:** Two of three sources say metal thread, so it was mostly gold or silver thread. Al-Watan says only sheikhs and emirs wore it, to stand apart. Saudipedia also names one plain *shatfa* covered only in white wool.
- **Speculation:** The camel-hair line may describe that plain white type, or the padding inside.
- **Design takeaway:** Borrow the square, many-sided shape, not the shine. Try a stiff square cord at a collar or bag handle, wrapped in matte thread the same colour as the cloth.

#### The incense frame (mudakhkhana)

- **Name:** No Saudi source found for the name. An Emirati source (Al-Bayan) calls it *mudakhkhana* or *mudakhkhanat al-oud*. No source used *mishkhab* or *mukhbara* for this tool.
- **How it was made:** Palm stalks (*arajeen*) soak about 15 days until soft. They are split into thin strips and wrapped around four palm-rib posts, tied with palm-fibre cord (*kanbar*). Today nylon cord is common. The result is an open cylinder with vents near the top.
- **How it was used:** A clay burner with coals, *oud* and *dukhoon* goes underneath. Clothes are spread over the frame to soak up the scent. Women made it.
- **Saudi link:** Saudipedia's *mabkhara* page says incense scented clothes, bodies and guests, and old burners were clay, metal or iron. It names no frame.
- **Speculation:** Saudi Gulf towns may have used the same tool, but no source says so yet.
- **Design takeaway:** Speculation: press a woven palm-strip pattern inside a collar stand. Or add a hidden, breathable inner patch that holds incense scent.

Sources: [Al-Bayan – The mudakhkhana](https://www.albayan.ae/five-senses/last-page/2018-04-08-1.3231883), [Saudipedia – The mabkhara](https://saudipedia.com/%D8%A7%D9%84%D9%85%D8%A8%D8%AE%D8%B1%D8%A9).

#### Palm-frond weaving (khoos)

- **What it is:** An old craft that weaves palm leaflets, best from male palms. It is done mostly by women. It is found in Asir, Qassim, Najran, Al-Ahsa and Madinah.
- **Local names:** In the west it is *khasaf*. Qassim makers are *ahl al-sifeef*. Al-Ahsa and Qatif call it *khoosiya*. Najran calls it *huwwak*.
- **How it was made:** Leaflets dry about two days in the sun. They are trimmed, bundled (*shalawi*), then soaked one to two hours before weaving. Tools are a long iron needle (*masalla*), scissors and an awl (*mikhraz*). Dyes such as *qirmiz* (red) and *neel* (indigo) came from herbalists.
- **Products:** Baskets (*zanabil*), food mats (*sufar*), fans (*mahaffat*), floor mats (*husr*), prayer mats and baby cots.
- **Design takeaway:** Weave a thin *khoos*-style braid in silk the same colour as the cloth. Use it as an inside hanger loop or belt loop, a detail only the owner sees.

Sources: [Saudipedia – Khoos craft](https://saudipedia.com/%D8%B5%D9%86%D8%A7%D8%B9%D8%A9-%D8%A7%D9%84%D8%AE%D9%88%D8%B5-%D9%81%D9%8A-%D8%A7%D9%84%D8%B3%D8%B9%D9%88%D8%AF%D9%8A%D8%A9).

#### Al-Ahsa bisht: the workshops today

- **What it is:** The Hasawi bisht is a loose robe, open at the front. It is made in 7 stages and takes 12 to 15 days, sometimes up to six months. The last stage, *al-bardakh*, uses a hammer to bring out the shine of the *zari* (metal thread).
- **Colors and patterns:** Zari comes in gold, yellow, silver, red or white. Black bishts are the most wanted, mainly for weddings. White is worn on Eid and for Friday prayer. Bisht types are named by the rows of decoration and their shape (triangle, square, hexagon, octagon): Malaki, Mukhawmas, Tabuq and others. Hasawi zari can be about 2.5 inches wide; Qatari zari about 1.5 inches.
- **The body cloth:** No source found on stripes woven into the body cloth. The cloth is often imported, for example Japanese fabric or wool woven in Iraq.
- **Who still makes it by hand:** Muhammad Ali Al-Qattan carries on his father's hand-sewing (Arab News, 2022). Husayn Al-Qattan sews by hand: one bisht takes 15 days and 8 craftsmen. He warns that machine bishts are sold as handmade (Al-Eqtisadiah, June 2025). Habib Mohammed, 60, has a small workshop in a Hofuf artisans' market and teaches his grandchildren. Other named families: Bou Ali, al-Marzouq, al-Hilal, al-Farhan, al-Hamad. The Al-Amir family has worked bisht metal thread for over 200 years.
- **Can you visit:** Yes, at the Hasawi Bisht Festival. The Heritage Commission runs it at Ibrahim Palace in Hofuf. The 3rd edition was in February 2026, with live workshops. No source confirmed a workshop open to visitors in Qaisariya souq.
- **Design takeaway:** Speculation: a very thin line of tone-on-tone zari only on the inside of the front edge, seen only when the garment moves. Al-Watan mentions a women's bisht stitched with incense burner and coffee pot motifs. For a men's piece, keep the hospitality idea but turn it into plain rows of triangles and squares, like men's bisht rows.

#### The shatfa agal

- **What it is:** Saudipedia says the *muqassab* is also called the *shatfah*, and "popularly known as the kings' egal." Another Saudipedia page describes the shatfah as a five-sided shape made of two layers, ten sides in all.

#### The Hijazi head wrap (ghabana)

- **What it is:** A turban worn in Makkah, Madinah and Jeddah. The colored kind is the *ghabana*, first made of patterned silk in saffron yellow. The best-known is the yellow "Halabi," made in Aleppo.
- **How it is built:** Al-Watan describes the "Alfi" turban: a base of thin palm wicker, covered in colored silk (yellow, red, black, green) and lined inside in blue or white silk. White gauze, several meters long, is wound around it. A small roll at the front holds the hanging tail (*adhaba*). The Ashraf wore a longer tail.
- **Who wore it and why:** Traders, scholars, teachers and the middle class. It was formal Hijazi dress, worn on Eid. One researcher says the first purpose of a head cover was shade from the sun. Village men wore a white ghutra with red edges and a black agal instead.
- **Debate and today:** One researcher says it came from India; another calls it core Hijazi heritage. Young men dropped it in the 1950s as not practical. It is still sold in Makkah markets.
- **Design takeaway:** Speculation: copy the hidden two-color lining (color outside, blue or white silk inside) as a quiet contrast lining in a collar or inner placket. A small saffron-yellow detail, seen only from inside, nods to the Halabi ghabana.

Sources: [Al-Watan – Alfi turban](https://www.alwatan.com.sa/article/1050048), [Al-Watan – ghabana debate](https://www.alwatan.com.sa/article/329019), [Al-Jazirah](https://www.al-jazirah.com/2019/20190401/wo2.htm), [Wikipedia – Hejazi turban](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hejazi_turban), [Saudipedia – Madinah garments](https://saudipedia.com/en/traditional-garments-of-al-madinah-al-munawwarah-province).

#### Jazan and Tihama: dress for the heat

- **Men:** The *izar* is a cloth wrapped around the waist, held by a belt. The *shemeez* is a long-sleeved shirt, open at the front. On the coast the outfit is two pieces of light cloth, suited to fishing and humid air. Older, well-off men add a black vest (*sudairiya*). Al-Dayer coffee farmers wear a one-color shirt, a wrap skirt (*wizrah*), a janbiya, and a headband of kadi, wormwood and jasmine.
- **Head covers:** The turban is the main one. Older men wear a cap of bamboo or cloth, wrapped with a turban. Jazan women make hats from palm fronds and doum palm, and caps from white cotton thread. No source confirmed local hat names.
- **Women:** A hand-embroidered *kurta* worn with long striped trousers. A colored embroidered head sheet (*muqanna'*) and a black head-and-shoulder cover (*muqallama*). Embroidery uses mountain green, sea blue and sand gold.
- **Why it suits the heat:** The coast uses light cloth for heat and humidity. The highland villages use heavy cotton for warmth.
- **Design takeaway:** Speculation: a wrap-front or side-tie closing from the izar, in light open-weave cotton, for summer. To make the women's kurta embroidery masculine, shrink it to one thin dark band at the hem. To make the men's jasmine headband feminine, use a small tone-on-tone jasmine motif.

Sources: [Saudipedia – Jazan folk dress (Arabic)](https://saudipedia.com/الأزياء-الشعبية-في-منطقة-جازان), [Arab News – wizrah and chemise](https://www.arabnews.com/saudi-arabia/wizrah-and-chemise-traditional-dress-of-al-dayer-coffee-bean-farmers-1795956), [Arab News – Jazan stitches](https://www.arabnews.com/node/2619545), [Al-Watan – Jazan palm crafts](https://www.alwatan.com.sa/ampArticle/394476).

#### Najdi painted doors

- **What it is:** Carved, painted wooden doors from Najd. They showed the family's identity and carried the maker's signature. Front doors often had the owner's name, the date, and words praising God.
- **Wood:** Palm trunks for the main door boards, because they are light. Athel (tamarisk) for the frame and beams; it is strong and stands up to weather. Later, wood from India replaced local tamarisk.
- **Patterns:** A round "sunflower" in the center, palm fronds, grapes, raindrops, *baythana* (small circles like cardamom seeds), *qeetan* (a braided band along the edges), and crowns. Also triangles for mountains and sun rays. Some designs were burned in with a hot iron.
- **Colors:** Green, yellow, red, blue, purple. Green came from acacia, yellow from acacia fruit. Later, bought powders were mixed with molasses, pomegranate peel and sugar; boiled pomegranate made the glue.
- **Who makes them today:** Each town had a master door painter. Today Abdullah Al-Khuzam makes Ha'il-style doors from athel in earth tones. Wrth runs a free 18-month Najdi woodwork course in Unayzah for Saudis.
- **Design takeaway:** Speculation: turn the *qeetan* braid into a tone-on-tone woven tape along a placket or hem. The tiny *baythana* circles could become stitched dots at seam ends. Keep colors muted, like a dull acacia green.

Sources: [Wrth – Najdi Doors booklet (PDF)](https://wrth.edu.sa/volumes/uploads/%D9%83%D8%AA%D9%8A%D8%A8-%D8%A7%D9%84%D8%A7%D9%94%D8%A8%D9%88%D8%A7%D8%A8-%D8%A7%D9%84%D9%86%D8%AC%D8%AF%D9%8A%D8%A9-Najdi-Doors.pdf), [Bayt Al Fann](https://www.baytalfann.com/post/decorated-doors-of-saudi-arabia), [Ithraeyat – The Art of Doors](https://ithraeyat.ithra.com/articles/the-art-of-doors), [Saudipedia – door decorations](https://saudipedia.com/en/traditional-door-decorations-in-saudi-arabia), [Arab News – Wrth](https://www.arabnews.com/node/2646178/saudi-arabia), [Al-Madina – Ha'il doors](https://www.al-madina.com/article/997310/).

#### The madas (zubayria) leather sandal

- **What it is:** A leather sandal with a big ring that wraps around the big toe. Arab News calls it *Madas Sharqi*, "sandal from the east," also *Zubairi*. A Saudi craft guide calls it *zubayria* and says it is found all over Saudi Arabia.
- **How it is made:** Camel, cow or sheep leather. Thick leather pieces are glued under the sole. Loops go on the sides and front. The sole is stitched in a decorative way. Old pairs used only leather and cotton thread; gold and silver thread came later.
- **Who wore it:** Saudi men in all areas, "from royalty to wealthy businessmen to ordinary people." It started in the central and eastern areas. Pairs from Qassim were called the best. In 2013, Arab News said women only began wearing it about ten years before.
- **Decoration:** Hand embroidery in colored threads, with colors that stand for each region.
- **Today:** In 2013 a Riyadh maker said most are now made in factories with imported leather. Only costly pairs are still made by hand, for royalty and rich men. The craft guide says old markets are still full of madas shops.
- **Design takeaway:** Borrow the decorated sole stitch: one line of tonal hand stitching along a hem or cuff edge. For women's pieces, keep the regional colored thread idea but in one quiet color. Speculation: a small leather zip pull shaped like the toe loop.

Sources: [Arab News – Traditional Saudi Madas (2013)](https://www.arabnews.com/art-culture/traditional-saudi-madas-458988), [EC-APR – Zubayria](https://encyclocraftsapr.com/zubayria/).

#### Henna and the Ghumrah night

- **What it is:** A dye from the henna tree. It starts dark red, turns golden yellow, and fades in one to three weeks.
- **When:** The night before a wedding is the "Henna Night" or *Ghumrah*. Women from both families meet at the bride's father's house to decorate her. Women also put on henna for Eid al-Fitr and Eid al-Adha.
- **Who made it:** The henna artist is *al-Muhanniyah* or *al-Naqqashah*, a traditional women's job. Some men use henna on their hair and beards.
- **Patterns:** *al-Jouri* for brides, *al-Misha'ab* for older women, *al-Qabdah* for children. Older women often cover the whole palm and sole. Young girls like fine designs in red or dark brown.
- **UNESCO:** In 2024, henna was added to UNESCO's list as a shared file of 16 Arab countries, Saudi Arabia among them.
- **Design takeaway:** Use henna's own color change, dark red to golden yellow, as a palette: a henna-brown inner collar band or lining. Because men use henna on beards, this red-brown already reads as a Saudi men's color too. Speculation: the sources name the patterns but do not show them, so do not copy a pattern until a *Muhanniyah* shows you one.

Sources: [Saudipedia – What is henna associated with](https://saudipedia.com/en/what-is-henna-associated-with-in-saudi-arabia), [Saudipedia – Henna](https://saudipedia.com/en/henna), [UNESCO – Henna (2024)](https://ich.unesco.org/en/RL/henna-rituals-aesthetic-and-social-practices-02116).

#### Ithmid kohl

- **What it is:** *Ithmid* is a kohl: a very fine powder of minerals for the eyes. It has a long history in the Arabian Peninsula. For Muslims it has special meaning, because the Prophet used it.
- **Today:** Young Saudis are bringing it back. A Saudi online shop (Athmad19) says real *ithmid* is not shiny. If the powder shines a lot, it is fake. Fakes can hold lead, iron or glass.
- **Health claims:** The article cites studies on eye benefits. These were not checked here.
- **Not found:** No source today on the kohl pot (*mukhula*).
- **Design takeaway:** Real *ithmid* is a matte black, not a shiny one. Use a soft, matte "ithmid black" instead of glossy jet black for an abaya or a thobe trim. Speculation: since the Prophet used it, the color can sit on men's pieces as easily as women's.

Sources: [Arab News – Reviving ithmid kohl (2024)](https://www.arabnews.pk/saudi-arabia/reviving-ithmid-kohl-the-intersection-of-health-beauty-and-culture-in-saudi-arabia-2567696).

#### Madinah women's dress and the bride's shara'a

- **Everyday dress:** The *kurta* (or *kerta*) runs from neck to feet. Its color was set by the woman's age. Young women wore bright silk.
- **Under and over:** A cotton vest with gold buttons, worn with pants of *al-Halabi*, a gold silk cloth.
- **Head and outdoors:** The *mehrama* head cover is cotton, plain or hand-decorated for weddings. Outdoors: the black *al-jawiya*, a linen or cotton *burqa* with an eye slit, and a black front-opening abaya.
- **The bride:** *al-Shara'a al-Medini* is a dark pink dress with gold thread. Other bridal dresses: *Zabon*, *Manthour*, *Mahaf*, *Ifrangi* and *Misk Shara'a*.
- **Men vs women:** On the same page, rich Madinah men's vests had silver buttons. The women's vest had gold buttons.
- **Design takeaway:** Make Madinah's split a house rule: silver-tone buttons on men's pieces, soft gold on women's. Use the bride's dark pink only as a hidden lining, so it stays an "if you know" detail.

Sources: [Saudipedia – Traditional garments of Al-Madinah Province](https://saudipedia.com/en/traditional-garments-of-al-madinah-al-munawwarah-province).

#### Eastern Province everyday menswear

- ***Izar:*** A cloth wrapped twice round the waist, for home, sleep or field work.
- ***Dishdasha:*** A white thobe with a chest button.
- ***Shalahat:*** A thobe with wider sleeves, for dignitaries and sheikhs. Wider sleeves meant rank.
- ***Al-Maksar:*** A knee-length shirt for craftsmen and farmers. Saudipedia says it "resembles an undershirt."
- **Over the thobe:** Bisht, *farwa* or *daghla*, for show or warmth.
- **Women:** The *dir'ah*; the *nafnof* (Indian style, silk or satin); the *Hashimi thobe* with gold embroidery; party dresses *al-Masrah*, *al-Manthur* and *al-Thuraya*.
- **Design takeaway:** The *maksar* is a ready base for a men's knee-length overshirt in the casual line. Borrow the *shalahat* idea for the formal line: a sleeve just slightly wider than normal. For women, the *maksar* shape in silk becomes a long shirt.

#### The skullcap under the ghutra (*gahfiyah*, *tagiyah*)

- **What it is:** A short, round cap worn under the ghutra or shemagh. A museum record calls it *gahfiyah* or *tagiyah*, and lists it for Saudi Arabia, the UAE, Bahrain, Kuwait, Qatar, Oman and Yemen.
- **What it is made of:** Soft white cotton, crocheted or knitted in a net with small holes. The museum piece is about 16 cm by 23 cm, with a geometric pattern.
- **Why it is worn:** It holds the head cloth in place. It keeps hair oil off the ghutra. The holes let air reach the scalp and keep it cool.
- **Who made it:** Older caps were "probably knitted by the women." Most new ones are factory-made and imported.
- **Not found:** No source today on a Saudi maker or a Saudi-only style.
- **Design takeaway:** The gahfiyah is a hidden piece that only the wearer sees. Use its open net pattern on the inside: a crocheted or pierced cotton lining panel at the back neck of a thobe or jacket, to let air in. For women, the same net can sit under a sheer sleeve or on a scarf edge. Speculation: a tiny round net patch inside the collar as a "you know" brand mark.

Sources: [Zay Initiative – Skullcap, UAE](https://thezay.org/product/26148), [Wikipedia – Taqiyah](<https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taqiyah_(cap)>).

#### Prayer beads (*subha*, *misbaha*) and black coral *yusr*

- **What it is:** A string of beads used for prayer and remembrance. Saudipedia calls it a traditional handicraft found across Saudi Arabia. The makers are called *Al-Subhiyyah*.
- **Where:** Mostly Makkah and Madinah, sold in Hajj and Umrah seasons. Jeddah's old Al-Subhiya (Al-Khaskiyah) market was where beads were made and sold. Riyadh has family shops too.
- **Materials:** Sandalwood, tamarind ("red wood"), agarwood, date and olive pits. The famous one is *yusr*: black coral from the Red Sea. It is soaked in water so it does not crack, then sometimes set with silver or lead. Amber is one of the most costly. Olive-pit beads may be soaked in oud oil.
- **How it is made:** Cut the raw material, shape it, drill it, sand it, polish it, then thread it. String is cotton, silk or nylon. One olive-pit set takes about five days by hand.
- **Counts:** Stone sets often have 33 beads, a head bead and two spacers. The full set is 99 beads, about 101 pieces in all.
- **Today:** The Red Sea Museum in Jeddah shows rare *yusr* beads, some set with silver or amber (April 2026). In 2023 a Riyadh maker said one amber set sold for about 70,000 riyals.
- **Design takeaway:** Men already carry beads as a quiet sign of taste. Use a single *yusr*-black or amber-colored bead as a zip pull, a cuff-link, or a button on a bisht-style coat. Or cut a hidden inside pocket sized for beads. For women, a strand of three small beads on a scarf tie. Speculation: a natural sandalwood or oud-scented button.

Sources: [Saudipedia – Al-Subha](https://saudipedia.com/en/al-subha-prayer-beads), [Saudipedia – The craft of Al-Subha](https://saudipedia.com/en/the-craft-of-al-subha-in-saudi-arabia), [Arab News – coral beads at Red Sea Museum (2026)](https://www.arabnews.pk/node/2641340), [Arab News – Riyadh rosary maker (2023)](https://www.arabnews.com/node/2289241/amp).

#### Rijal Almaa village, Asir

- **What it is:** A stone village in a valley about 45–50 km west of Abha. Sources call it more than 900 years old. It sat on a trade road linking Yemen to the Levant through Makkah and Madinah.
- **The houses:** About 60 tall buildings of stone, clay and wood. Some reach eight floors. Many were once forts.
- **The look:** Fronts set with white quartz stone. Windows and doors are wood, painted in green, white, yellow and red shapes. The same shapes appear on mats and chairs in the yards.
- **Inside:** Rooms painted by the village women in Al-Qatt Al-Asiri (see Day 1).
- **The museum:** The Rijal Alma'a Heritage Museum sits in Al Al-wan Palace, over four centuries old. It holds more than 2,000 objects and papers in 19 sections: manuscripts, tools and weapons. No source lists clothes or jewelry there.
- **Status:** On UNESCO's tentative list (Saudi Arabia was cleared to file in 2018). One tourism site says it is on the full list; other sources do not back that up.
- **Design takeaway:** Take the white quartz line set into dark stone: a thin off-white piping or a row of white stitches on a dark charcoal or brown garment. For men, keep it to one line at a pocket or yoke. For women, use the four window colors in a tiny inside tab or on a lining only. Speculation: a rough-textured weave in stone grey with small white slubs, like quartz in a wall.

Sources: [Wikipedia – Rijal Almaa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rijal_Almaa), [Arab News 2022](https://www.arabnews.com/lifestyle/exploring-saudi-arabia-rijal-alma-village-is-the-jewel-in-asir-regions-crown-2128601), [Gulf News 2025](https://gulfnews.com/world/gulf/saudi/saudi-arabias-rijal-almaa-shines-as-a-heritage-and-tourism-icon-1.500255663), [Discover Aseer](https://discoveraseer.com/en/destinations/rijal-almaa).

#### Kohl pots (*makhala*) and the UNESCO listing

- **Big news:** In December 2025, Arabic kohl was added to UNESCO's list of world living heritage. Saudi Arabia was one of nine countries that put it forward.
- **The pot's name:** Writers on the subject call the pot *makhala* and the stick *merwad* ("needle"). UNESCO's text says the pots are "often passed down as family keepsakes." (This answers part of Day 6's question on the *mukhula*.)
- **Materials:** Silver or copper in some areas, where the pot counts as jewelry. Elsewhere leather, wood, reed or cloth, trimmed with beads, shells, buttons and tassels. Omani silver flasks have a silver stick on a silver chain.
- **Men's pots:** Men carried pots in male shapes: silver "bullet" shapes, or small powder-horn shapes.
- **Who and why:** Men, women and children wore kohl. It guarded the eyes from wind, sand and sun. Kohl was mostly made at home by women. *Ithmid* stone was a common gift brought back from Hajj.
- **Not found:** No source today shows a Saudi-only kohl pot shape.
- **Design takeaway:** The kohl pot is a feminine object with a ready masculine form: the slim silver "bullet." Use that shape as a metal tip on a drawstring, a zip pull, or a pen-pocket loop on a men's thobe. For women, use the stick-on-a-chain as a fine chain closure on an abaya front.

Sources: [Bedouin Silver – Arabic kohl and UNESCO (2025)](https://bedouinsilver.com/arabic-kohl-cultural-heritage-unesco/), [UNESCO – Arabic kohl](https://ich.unesco.org/en/RL/arabic-kohl-02261), [Zay Initiative – Kohl containers](https://thezay.org/kohl-containers).

#### The black goat-hair tent (*bayt al-sha'ar*)

- **What it is:** The "house of hair", the tent of desert families. It is still made in the north, in Al-Jouf and the Northern Borders.
- **Made of:** Goat hair and sheep wool. A 1966 AramcoWorld article says black or brown goat hair or sheep wool, "not, as many believe, from camel's hair." Saudipedia and Arab News also list camel hair, cotton and Sadu bands. So the sources differ on camel hair.
- **Who makes it:** Women. They weave long strips on a ground loom of palm or jujube wood, while talking, singing and reciting poetry. Strips up to 8 m long are sewn side by side. One tent takes three days to a week.
- **How it works:** In rain the hair swells and the gaps close, so water stays out (Saudipedia). In heat the side walls (*ruaq*) are rolled up. On freezing winter nights the long curtains are drawn shut.
- **Inside:** A woven curtain (*qata*) splits the tent. One side is open, for men and guests. The other is closed, for women. Curtains carry Sadu shapes: the eye, the rib, horse teeth, the tree.
- **Sizes:** Tents are named by how many poles they have, from one pole up to six (*al-musaddas*).
- **Today:** Women in the Northern Borders sell tents for SR1,600 to SR6,000 (Arab News, 2024).
- **Design takeaway:** Plain dark outside, one Sadu band hidden inside, like the tent curtain no stranger sees. For men: a charcoal-brown coat with one Sadu strip only on the inside facing. For women: a hidden inner panel in a wide abaya with one Sadu "eye." Speculation: a dense, slightly hairy brushed wool in tent black-brown for a winter bisht.

Sources: [Ithraeyat – Bayt al-sha'ar](https://ithraeyat.ithra.com/editions/sustainability/bayt-al-sha-ar), [AramcoWorld 1966 – The black tent](https://archive.aramcoworld.com/issue/196603/the.black.tent.htm), [Saudipedia – Bayt al-sha'ar (Arabic)](https://saudipedia.com/ar/بيت-الشعر-في-السعودية), [Arab News 2024 – Northern Borders weavers](https://www.arabnews.pk/node/2578922/saudi-arabia), [Arab News 2024 – Jouf](https://www.arabnews.com/saudi-arabia/bouyout-al-shaar-a-symbol-of-the-desert-that-brings-together-families-and-friends-in-saudi-arabias-jouf-2448741).

#### Khawlani coffee of Jazan

- **What it is:** Coffee grown by families in Jazan's mountains. UNESCO listed the know-how of growing it in 2022.
- **Where:** On terraces 1,000–1,800 m high. The name comes from the tribes of Khawlan bin Amir. Areas include Ad-Dayer Bani Malik, Fayfa, Al-Aridhah, Al-Raith, Al-Edabi and Harub.
- **The colors:** The fruit turns green, then yellow, orange, and red when ripe. It is picked one by one with a twist. It dries on rooftops until the seeds turn black. The husk is cracked with a long stone roller.
- **Who does what:** Boys work with their fathers: planting, drying, pruning, fixing terraces. Girls work with their mothers: picking, peeling, grinding.
- **What farmers wear:** A 2022 Gulf News story shows a 90-year-old farmer in a dark shirt, an ankle-length wrap skirt (*wizrah*) and a dagger belt. His son wears a flower headdress.
- **Size:** 724 listed farmers in the UNESCO file (2019). By the end of 2021: 400,000 trees on 600 farms, about 800 tonnes a year (Gulf News).
- **Design takeaway:** Use the ripening run — green, yellow, orange, red — as four tiny stitches inside a collar. Or use "dried bean" near-black brown for a winter thobe or overshirt. For men, one color only. For women, the four-step run on a cuff lining. Speculation: a stepped quilting line on a jacket back, like the terraces.

Sources: [UNESCO – Khawlani coffee](https://ich.unesco.org/en/RL/knowledge-and-practices-related-to-cultivating-khawlani-coffee-beans-01863), [UNESCO nomination file](https://ich.unesco.org/doc/src/45434-EN.pdf), [Saudi UNESCO delegation](https://ksaforunesco.org/heritages/knowledge-and-practices-related-to-cultivating-khawlani-coffee-beans/), [Gulf News 2022](https://gulfnews.com/world/gulf/saudi/saudi-coffee-legacy-percolates-through-generations-1.85598140), [Arab News 2022](https://www.arabnews.com/saudi-arabia/unesco-adds-saudi-khawlani-coffee-camel-hedaa-to-intangible-cultural-heritage-list-2209201).

#### Al-Bahah dress

- **Why it looks this way:** Saudipedia says the clothes were shaped by a simple life and little cloth in the past.
- **Men:** A thobe of yellowish cotton called *mubrim*, from twisted cotton thread. A man had one thobe for work and one for feasts. A wool turban (*al-khayaliyah*) in red, navy, green or brown.
- **The shared bisht:** The bisht was rare and costly, so one family shared one. The eldest wore it when they went out together. The local cloak cloth, *al-bidi*, was very thick sheep wool.
- **Women:** Black, wide, loose dresses, tied at the waist. A white belt was worn against the black. Everyday belts were colored cloth. Chest and sides were embroidered by hand.
- **Feast dress:** The *al-mashal* thobe. The name means "something stored until needed."
- **Head:** A light black shayla, wrapped around the head and under the neck, with a piece of yellow cloth on top.
- **Cloth:** Shiny black percale, satin and crepe.
- **Design takeaway:** The white belt on black: a thin off-white waist tape inside a black abaya. For men, flip it: a black tape inside a white thobe. The yellow on top of the black shayla: a thin saffron edge on a women's scarf; for men, a yellow under-collar on a dark bisht. "Stored until needed" is a good name idea for a feast line.

Source: [Saudipedia – Traditional garments of Al-Bahah](https://saudipedia.com/en/traditional-garments-of-al-bahah-province).

#### What men wore for the Ardah

- **The list (Saudipedia, 2023):** Bright colors and embroidery mark Ardah clothes.
- ***Al-Murowdin* ("Abu Rudon"):** The robe with long, wide sleeves. In the southwest it is called *al-midhial* or *al-mufraj*.
- ***Al-Sayah*:** A thin over-robe, open at the front, white or light yellow. Worn in summer.
- ***Al-Zaboon*:** An ankle-long robe, open all down the front. Buttons only on the upper part. Small slits at both sides.
- ***Al-Mazwiyah*:** An old heavy, rough cloak, often of hair. Black or red-brown, with no pattern.
- ***Al-Jukhah*:** A jubba of soft wool cloth called *mahoor*. Best known in red, also black, blue, and rarely green.
- **Head:** The *ghutrah al-shal* of shalki wool, white or brown, from India and Pakistan. The finest kind, *tharma*, is soft, light and a little yellow. Under it, a white cotton cap.
- ***Al-Maqtaa*:** Like a thobe but wider at the bottom, made of many pieces of cloth.
- **Drummers:** Wear the *al-farmaliyah*, a fancy velvet top with long sleeves.
- **Design takeaway:** The red *jukhah*: a men's winter overshirt in deep red soft wool, with no trim at all. The *zaboon*: buttons on the top half only, plus small side slits, for a long modern shirt. The *tharma*: a warm, slightly yellow off-white for scarves. For women, take the *maqtaa* idea of many pieces as quiet panel seams.

Source: [Saudipedia – Traditional costumes for the Saudi Ardah](https://saudipedia.com/en/article/1757/culture/performing-arts/what-are-the-traditional-costumes-for-the-saudi-ardah).

#### Hail women's dress (the 2021 paper)

- **The paper is found:** Hend Amen and Hanaa Saad Al Mermesh, *International Design Journal*, vol. 11, issue 5, September 2021. The full PDF is free online.
- **Garments:** The *maqta'* (or *dira'a*): ankle-long, long sleeves, like a man's thobe, with a pocket and an inner pleat (*khubna*). The *kurta*: the town version, with a center slit and wide neck. The *masrah*: a very wide robe over the rest. The *zaboun*: open front, closed only at the top, with metal-cord (*zari*) loops. The abaya, for going out.
- **Head:** The black *shayla* (or *ghadfa*), worn at all times, even at home. A band (*isaba*) over it. A face veil (*litham*).
- **Cloth:** Cotton every day. Silk only for feasts, and women owned few silk pieces. Wool for the abaya. Named cloths include *roz*, *melisan*, *bajdi* (like velvet), *shalki* wool, and *zarbaft* silk.
- **Colors:** Red in all shades, purple, brown, green and blue. Plain white was avoided because it looked like men's dress. Patterned white was fine. Old names: *ashhab* for grey, *soli asfar* for orange.
- **Why:** The *masrah* kept the body covered, suited the heat, and suited field work. In winter, thin layers were stacked for warmth.
- **Also:** Okaz (2026) names the *qilani* abaya, made in small amounts in Hail and Diriyah, but mostly brought from Iraq.
- **Still missing:** Hail men's dress. No source found.
- **Design takeaway:** In Hail, plain white meant men. So for women, use white only with a pattern: a white-on-white jacquard. For men, borrow the women's *ashhab* grey. The hidden inner pleat (*khubna*) can become a hidden back pleat for ease in a men's thobe.

Sources: [International Design Journal – Hail paper](https://idj.journals.ekb.eg/article_191700.html), [full PDF (Arabic)](https://idj.journals.ekb.eg/article_191700_769dd8e203da55eeb2d51ced5bc5919f.pdf?lang=ar), [Okaz 2026](https://www.okaz.com.sa/local/na/2237252).

## Track 2 — Fabric school

Your belief that "polyester = cheap" is half wrong. Polyester is cheap when it is used to *replace* a better fiber. It is luxury-grade when it does a job natural fibers can't, like holding a pleat or making a tough shell. The rule: every fiber in a garment must earn its place.

### Lesson 1 — The two fiber families

| Family | Examples | Comes from | Big strength | Big weakness |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Natural, plant | Cotton, linen | Cotton plant, flax stalk | Breathes, soft, cool | Wrinkles, shrinks |
| Natural, animal | Wool, cashmere, silk, camel hair | Sheep, goats, silkworms, camels | Warm when cold, cool when warm, drapes well | Needs care, pricier |
| Made from wood pulp | Viscose, modal, lyocell, cupro | Trees, processed | Silky drape, cheaper than silk | Weaker when wet |
| Made from oil | Polyester, nylon (polyamide), elastane (spandex), acrylic | Petroleum | Strong, holds shape, stretches, dries fast | Doesn't breathe, traps sweat smell |

Cupro was made to replace silk, acrylic to copy wool, polyester to replace cotton. Source: [Nona Source (LVMH textile platform)](https://www.nona-source.com/blogs/news/why-are-synthetic-and-artificial-textiles-used-in-luxury-fashion).

### Lesson 2 — Woven vs knit, and the three basic weaves

- **Woven:** threads cross over and under at right angles. Firm, doesn't stretch much, edges fray. Shirts, trousers, suits.
- **Knit:** one thread looped like a chain. Stretches and springs back, edges curl. T-shirts, sweaters, jersey.
- **Quick test:** pull it. A knit stretches and bounces back; a woven stays firm.
- **Plain weave:** over one, under one. Crisp, strong, same on both sides. Most dress shirts; also Al Sadu.
- **Twill:** makes diagonal lines. Softer drape, hides stains, resists wrinkles. Denim, chinos, most suit cloth.
- **Satin:** threads "float" over 4+ others, so it shines. Smooth but weaker. Made with cotton it's called **sateen**.

Sources: [Sew Guide](https://sewguide.com/woven-fabrics/), [Comfort World](https://www.comfortworld.co.uk/long-live-clothes/dare-to-wear-different-types-of-fabric-and-what-they-can-do.html).

### Lesson 3 — Why linen is cool (your example, confirmed)

Linen comes from the flax stalk. Each fiber has a hollow channel inside, so air moves and sweat leaves fast. It is usually woven loosely, which adds airflow. Cotton breathes too but holds sweat longer. The cost: linen wrinkles because the fiber doesn't bend back.

Sources: [Cable Melbourne](https://www.cablemelbourne.com/blogs/news/what-is-linen), [MasterClass](https://www.masterclass.com/articles/what-is-cotton-and-what-is-linen).

### Lesson 4 — Blends, and why each one exists

| Blend | Why it exists | Where it fits |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 65% polyester / 35% cotton | Tough, cheap, few wrinkles, little shrink | Uniforms, workwear. Not luxury. |
| Cotton-heavy (e.g. 65 cotton / 35 poly) | Keeps cotton's softness, adds strength | Mid-market shirts |
| 97% wool / 3% elastane | Wool look with comfort stretch | Trousers, soft tailoring |
| \~50 wool / 48 poly / 2 elastane | Cuts cost, adds durability | High-street suits (M&S example) |
| Wool / silk / linen | Wool stops linen wrinkling; silk adds shine; linen adds coolness | Luxury summer jackets (Loro Piana, Zegna) |

- In one Loro Piana "Summertime" cloth, merino wool is 71% and silk 15%. Wool being the majority cuts linen's creasing a lot.
- Poly-cotton blends breathe less than pure cotton and can feel sticky in heat. That matters a lot in Saudi summers.

Sources: [Gelato on poly-cotton](https://www.gelato.com/blog/cotton-polyester-blends-guide), [Truro Fabrics](https://trurofabrics.com/products/stretch-wool-suiting-fabric-black), [M&S trousers](https://www.marksandspencer.com/black-textured-tailored-fit-trousers/p/clp60205839), [Bonne Gueule](https://bonnegueule.fr/en-eu/blogs/medias/le-retour-de-nos-costumes-dete-en-tissu-laine-lin-et-soie), [Permanent Style](https://www.permanentstyle.com/2017/03/a-guide-to-wool-silk-linens-mixes-colours-bunches), [Villa 88](https://www.villa88.com/man/sharp-under-the-sun-modern-mens-suit-built-for-the-heat/).

### Lesson 5 — Spandex (elastane): how much is too much

- **2–3%** is the normal "comfort stretch" in tailored wool.
- A 2024 study of suit fabrics found **7% or more** elastane in the cross threads can cause seam puckering (wavy seams). Light, loose fabrics with elastane stretched too much to tailor well.
- In knits (activewear, swim) elastane goes much higher, because the goal is a tight fit that snaps back.
- **Rule for you:** tailoring 2–3% max. More than that and it starts looking like activewear.

Sources: [Journal of Natural Fibers, 2024](https://doaj.org/article/542e21882932402fb39206625ef5a6d2), [Croft Mill](https://www.croftmill.co.uk/products/tango-stretch-wool-elastane-dress-fabric).

### Lesson 6 — How luxury houses actually use synthetics

- **Prada nylon:** Miuccia Prada turned a nylon her grandfather used for trunk covers into backpacks in 1979. Prada uses about 700,000 meters a year. In 2019 it launched **Re-Nylon** with Aquafil's ECONYL yarn, made from fishing nets, carpet and other waste, and aimed to switch all virgin nylon by end of 2021. Lesson: they made the synthetic the *signature* and gave it a story.
- **Pleats:** polyester holds a pleat in place because heat can set its shape. Natural fibers can't do this as well.
- **Organza:** silk is the luxury version; polyester and nylon copies are common. Poly organza doesn't breathe.
- **Finish matters:** LVMH's suppliers work to make synthetic fabrics feel almost the same as natural ones.

Sources: [Dazed](https://www.dazeddigital.com/fashion/article/45002/1/miuccia-prada-sustainable-re-nylon-aquafil), [FashionNetwork](https://in.fashionnetwork.com/news/Prada-to-use-nylon-made-from-recycled-plastic,1113272.html), [Nona Source](https://www.nona-source.com/blogs/news/why-are-synthetic-and-artificial-textiles-used-in-luxury-fashion), [Aza Fashions on organza](https://www.azafashions.com/blog/what-is-organza-fabric-guide/).

### Lesson 7 — Reading quality numbers

- **Super numbers (wool):** describe how thin each wool fiber is, not how heavy the cloth is. Super 100s ≈ 18.5 microns; Super 150s ≈ 16–16.5 microns. Higher = softer but weaker. Super 100s–130s is right for clothes worn often. Finishing and the mill matter as much as the number.
- **GSM (grams per square meter):** how heavy the fabric is. Rough guide: summer tee 120–160; regular tee 160–190; shirts 130–170; suiting 240–320.
- **Neither number alone means quality.** A good 130 GSM cotton shirt can beat a badly made 180 GSM one.

Sources: [Enzo Custom](https://enzocustom.com/journal/understanding-super-numbers-in-fabrics-the-key-to-quality-and-comfort), [Edit Suits](https://www.editsuits.com/blogs/news/guide-to-suit-fabric-weights-super-100s-150s), [Siyaram's](https://www.siyaram.com/blogs/?p=851), [SANVT](https://sanvt.com/blogs/journal/fabric-weight-a-guide-to-gsm).

### Rules for A'ila Areeqa (from today's research)

1. **Next to skin in Saudi heat: natural only.** Cotton, linen, silk, fine wool. Polyester traps heat and sweat.
2. **Synthetics only where they do a job:** a weatherproof outer shell, a pleat that must stay, a lining that slides, 2–3% stretch in a trouser.
3. **Tell the fiber's story** if you use a synthetic, like Prada did. Recycled nylon with a reason is luxury; random polyester is not.
4. **Heritage link:** camel hair and goat wool were the original Saudi luxury fibers (bisht, Sadu). A camel-hair or wool-silk blend outer layer is your most "if you know, you know" fabric choice.

### Questions to ask any fabric supplier

- [ ] Exact fiber percentages, and is it woven or knit?
- [ ] GSM, and Super number if wool?
- [ ] Which mill made it, and in what country?
- [ ] How much does it shrink after washing?
- [ ] For stretch: % elastane and how much it stretches each way?

#### Lesson 8 — Silk types and momme

Silk types differ in weave, weight and shine. Those three decide how a silk feels in the heat.

- **Momme (mm) is silk's weight.** Higher number = heavier cloth. One momme is about 4.3 g/m², so 16 momme is about 69 g/m².
- **Heat:** One silk maker says shiny silks show sweat, and heavy, dense weaves trap heat and cling. It says open plain weaves of about 7–14 momme let heat out best. This is one company's advice, not lab data.

| Silk | Look | Typical momme | Cling | Hot-climate fit |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Habotai | Plain, soft sheen | 8–16 | Low | Good (light plain weave) |
| Crepe de chine | Twisted yarn, matte, pebbly | 12–16 (up to \~30) | Less than charmeuse | Speculation: good, matte may hide sweat |
| Georgette | Sheer, crinkly | 5–10 | Floaty | Speculation: good for loose layers |
| Charmeuse | Glossy face, dull back | 12–30 | Clings | Weak: shows sweat |
| Silk twill | Fine diagonal lines | 16–19 | Medium | Speculation: medium |

- **Rule for A'ila Areeqa:** For hot-weather abayas and linings, pick light, matte, plain or crepe silks under about 16 momme. Keep glossy charmeuse for evening pieces or small accents.

Sources: [Silk World – Silk weights](https://www.silkworld.com.au/blogs/curated-edits/silk-weights-and-measures), [Mulberry Park Silks – What is momme](https://mulberryparksilks.com/blogs/mulberry/what-is-momme-in-silk-fabric), [Sewing.org – Silk terms](https://www.sewing.org/html/silkterms.html), [Minerva – Silk types compared](https://minervapatterns.com/blog/silk-types-compared-charmeuse-habotai-dupioni-and-crepe), [Murier Silk – Momme guide](https://muriersilk.com/blog/silk-momme-weight-guide), [Anuprerna – Is silk good for hot weather](https://anuprerna.com/blogs/is-silk-good-for-hot-weather-a-summer-fabric-analysis/163120936), [Dharma Trading – Crepe de chine](https://dharmatrading.com/fabric/silk/cdc.html).

#### Lesson 9 — Cashmere, camel hair and vicuña grades

Thinner and longer fibers are better. The "micron" number tells you how thin a fiber is.

- **Micron:** the width of one fiber. A human hair is about 100. Under US rules, cashmere must average under 19 microns.
- **Cashmere grades (one retailer's system, not official):** A is about 14–15.5 microns, longest fibers. B is 16–19. C is over 20, short and pills. The best fibers are up to about 50 mm long.
- **Where from:** China makes about 70% of the world's cashmere, Mongolia about 20%. Loro Piana says goats shed their winter undercoat March to May, and its first processing is at its own mill in Ulaanbaatar. Its baby cashmere comes from goat kids in Inner Mongolia.
- **Camel hair:** Fine camel hair is the soft undercoat of the two-humped Bactrian camel, mostly from China and Mongolia. The UN's FAO gives adult down as 19–24 microns and baby camel about 19. Loro Piana sells baby camel cloth, but no Loro Piana page explains how it sources it. Treat "first combing" claims as marketing until a supplier puts them in writing.
- **Why not Arabian camel hair:** A museum database says one-humped camel hair is poor and rarely used. The FAO says camels in the hottest deserts grow coarser down.
- **Vicuña:** about 12.5 microns, from wild animals in the Andes above 4,000 m.
- **Rule for A'ila Areeqa:** For a bisht-style coat, get micron number, fiber length and country in writing from every mill. Buy Bactrian camel or cashmere from Mongolia or Inner Mongolia. Never sell Arabian camel hair as fine cloth.

Sources: [Gentleman's Gazette – Cashmere explained](https://www.gentlemansgazette.com/cashmere-explained/), [Robert Old – Grades of cashmere](https://www.robertold.co.uk/pages/understanding-the-different-grades-of-cashmere), [Loro Piana – Cashmere](https://us.loropiana.com/en/about-loro-piana/savoirfaire/fibers/cashmere), [Loro Piana – Baby Cashmere](https://us.loropiana.com/en/about-loro-piana/savoirfaire/fibers/baby-cashmere), [Loro Piana – Vicuña](https://us.loropiana.com/en/our-world/vicuna), [FAO – Camel hair](https://www.fao.org/4/v9384e/v9384e05.htm), [CAMEO (MFA Boston) – Camel hair](https://cameo.mfa.org/wiki/Camel_hair), [Britannica – Camel hair](https://www.britannica.com/topic/camel-hair).

#### Lesson 10 — Linings

The lining touches the body. In heat, a lining that lets sweat out matters more than shine.

| Lining | Breathes | Lasts | Cost | Notes |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Cupro (Bemberg) | Best | Good | Higher | Made by Asahi Kasei from cotton seed fuzz; smooth, anti-static |
| Viscose ("Ermazine") | Good | Tough | Mid | Savile Row favorite; more colors |
| Acetate | Light | Weak | Low | "Much flimsier"; not a good cheap swap |
| Polyester | Poor | Strong | Lowest | Feels "like a sauna"; sign of a cheap suit |
| Silk | Good to fair | Weakest | 5x or more | Wears out fast; few top tailors use it |

- **How much lining for heat:** Unlined lets in the most air but can rumple. Half-lined (back only) lets heat out and still hangs well. Permanent Style says light cloth can be fully lined unless it is over about 35°C or very humid. Keep sleeves lined so the jacket slides on.
- **Rule for A'ila Areeqa:** Bemberg cupro, never polyester. Half-line or unline summer coats, keep sleeves lined. Gulf summers pass 35°C, so half-lined is the default.

#### Lesson 11 — How a jacket or coat is built

What is inside decides how it hangs and how long it lasts. Glue is the cheap way. Stitched canvas is the quality way.

- **Full canvas:** horsehair canvas sewn through the whole front. It shapes to the body over time. Costs most.
- **Half canvas:** canvas only in the chest and lapel; the rest is glued.
- **Fused:** glue only. Cheapest, can look stiff, and the glue can "bubble" after cleaning. That cannot be fixed.
- **Pinch test:** pinch the sleeve to feel normal thickness. Then pinch below the lowest buttonhole and gently pull. A loose third layer = canvas. Stiff with no separate layer = fused.
- **Seams:** *French seam* hides raw edges inside a second seam. *Flat-felled* is folded twice and stitched flat, very strong. *Hong Kong (bound) seam* wraps each edge in bias lining, for unlined pieces and silk. *Overlocked* is the usual mass-market machine edge.
- **Stitches per inch:** brands say 12–14 for fine tops, 8–10 for fast fashion. No tailoring source gives a set number for jackets.
- **Hand vs machine:** a hand pick stitch along the lapel wanders slightly; a machine (AMF) makes it perfectly even. A hand buttonhole takes 30–45 minutes and looks raised and uneven on the back. Good garments leave about 1 inch of extra cloth in seams so a tailor can let them out.
- **Rule for A'ila Areeqa:** Coats and bishts at least half canvas, never fused. Hong Kong seams inside unlined summer pieces. Ask for 12+ stitches per inch. Hand buttonholes on the top line.

Sources: [Art of Manliness – Fused vs canvassed](https://www.artofmanliness.com/STYLE/CLOTHING/THE-ART-OF-MANLINESS-SUIT-SCHOOL-PART-I-FUSED-VS-CANVASSED-SUITS/), [Gentleman's Gazette – How to spot a cheap suit](https://www.gentlemansgazette.com/how-to-spot-a-cheap-suit/), [Eileen Fisher – Anatomy of a seam](https://www.eileenfisher.com/a-sustainable-life/journal/repair-care/anatomy-of-a-seam.html?loc=CA), [Kopal – Quality vs quantity](https://kopalshop.com/blogs/journal/quality-v-s-quantity), [Husbands Paris – The pick stitch](https://husbands-paris.com/en/the-pick-stitch), [Enzo Custom – Threading the needle](https://enzocustom.com/journal/threading-the-needle), [Articles of Style – Quality suit](https://articlesofstyle.com/blogs/news/how-to-spot-a-quality-suit).

#### Lesson 12 — Natural dyeing today

A few companies still dye with plants at real scale. It costs more and varies more batch to batch than synthetic dye.

- **Japan:** Tokushima makes most of Japan's *sukumo*, indigo leaves fermented about 100 days; the whole process takes almost a year. BUAISOU grows, ferments and dyes. A Tokushima mill, Rampuya, dyes most of Japan's natural-indigo selvedge denim.
- **USA:** Stony Creek Colors grows indigo with family farms. Its liquid dye is made with Archroma, and Levi's tested it.
- **India:** Aura Herbal Textiles (Ahmedabad) dyes with madder, pomegranate and turmeric; in 2010 it said 5,000+ metres a day (old figure).
- **Europe:** Tintoremus in Spain grows about 10 hectares of indigo.
- **Downsides:** color shifts with season, soil and water. No standard shade cards like Pantone. Many natural dyes fade unless a mordant (fixing salt like alum) is used. Madder needs 100–200 g of root per kg of cloth. Natural dyes barely work on polyester.
- **Luxury use:** Sies Marjan made a silk collection with Cornell's Natural Dye Studio. No major luxury house was found dyeing a regular line naturally.
- **Rule for A'ila Areeqa:** Natural indigo or madder works as a small, special line on cotton, linen or silk. Ask every dyer for written ISO 105 colorfastness results, including sweat. Tell customers small shade differences are normal.

Sources: [Tokushima Prefecture – Awa ai](https://www.pref.tokushima.lg.jp/en/japanese/natural_culture/traditional_culture/awa-ai), [Cool Hunting – BUAISOU](https://coolhunting.com/design/studio-visit-buaisou-brooklyn-japanese-sukumo-indigo-dyeing), [Just Style – Archroma and Stony Creek](https://www.just-style.com/news/archroma-stony-creek-to-scale-plant-based-pre-reduced-indigo), [Business Today – Aura Herbal Textiles](https://www.businesstoday.in/magazine/special/story/aura-herbal-textiles-dyeing-for-life-15816-2010-10-13), [FashionUnited – Tintoremus](https://fashionunited.uk/news/fashion/from-field-to-fashion-tintoremus-homegrown-indigo-cultivation-promotes-natural-dyes/2025081283334), [Fibre2Fashion – Natural dye quality](https://www.fibre2fashion.com/industry-article/11033/the-void-in-quality-control-lack-of-standardised-practices-and-colour-reproducibility-for-natural-dyeing), [Open Access Government – Fastness](https://www.openaccessgovernment.org/article/the-challenges-around-the-fastness-of-natural-dyes-for-textiles/199144/), [WWD – Sies Marjan and Cornell](https://wwd.com/business-news/business-features/cornell-sies-marjan-fall-2020-nyfw-natural-dye-botanical-silks-1203502532/).

#### Lesson 13 — Color tests: sweat, rubbing and sun

Sweat, rubbing and sun can each move or fade dye. Labs score the damage from 5 (best) to 1 (worst). Ask for the numbers in writing, not "good fastness".

- **What the grades mean:** A grey scale has pairs of grey chips, one per grade. At 5 the two chips match: no change. Lower grades show a bigger gap. Half steps (like 3-4) sit in between. One score is for color loss, one for staining (dye moving onto white cloth).
- **The sweat tests:** ISO 105-E04 uses two fake sweats, acid and alkaline, at 37 °C. AATCC 15 (the US test) uses acid only. The cloth is pressed against a white strip of mixed fibers, then graded.
- **What buyers ask for:** A draft Saudi (SASO) textile rule lists acid sweat fastness at 3-4. One US retailer asks for sweat color change 4 and staining 3; rubbing dry 4 and wet 3. Woolmark asks for light fastness 4 on darker shades. No luxury house's spec was found in public.
- **Rubbing (ISO 105-X12):** Wet rubbing is the weak spot for dark colors. One lab says medium and dark cotton often falls below 2-3 when wet. A dye maker linked poor results to hard water in the dye bath.
- **Sun (ISO 105-B02):** Fading is judged against eight blue wool samples, from 1 (weakest) to 8 (strongest).
- **Silk and black abaya cloth:** Some silk dyes bleed with sweat under the arms. Silk is also hurt by alkaline things. Speculation: for silk, ask for the alkaline sweat result too. For black abaya cloth, ask for wet rubbing, tested on the same dye lot you will buy.
- **Rule for A'ila Areeqa:** Every dark or silk fabric needs a lab report: sweat (acid and alkaline) at least 3-4, rubbing dry 4 and wet 3, light 4.

#### Lesson 14 — Hiding sweat marks

Wet cloth looks darker because a thin layer of water sends light back into the fabric. So colors that change most when darkened show sweat most. Most sources here are brand blogs; none cites a lab test.

| Shows sweat less | Shows sweat more |
| --- | --- |
| Black, deep navy, deep charcoal | Mid-tones: grey, blue, olive, beige |
| White, cream | Pastels (can also turn see-through when damp) |
| Bold prints and patterns | Light grey |

- **Black is not perfect:** One brand says black can still show shiny damp patches and white salt rings.
- **Matte vs shiny:** Sand-washing gives silk a matte, suede-like surface. Speculation: matte may hide damp shine better than satin. No source tested this.
- **Moisture-wicking:** Wicking spreads sweat outward so it dries faster. Brands disagree. Two undershirt brands say wicking pushes sweat to the outside, where it shows. Another says it helps stop stains.
- **Silk under the arms:** Sweat and deodorant can stain and weaken silk for good. Underarm shields help. Let deodorant dry before dressing.
- **Rule for A'ila Areeqa:** For hot-weather pieces, choose very dark, white or patterned cloth. Avoid mid-grey and pastel.

Sources: [Live Science](https://www.livescience.com/62604-why-wet-fabric-is-darker.html), [Numi](https://wearnumi.com/blogs/women-undershirts/what-color-hides-sweat-the-best), [Ejis](https://www.ejisinc.com/blogs/news/which-colors-show-sweat-the-least), [Thompson Tee](https://thompsontee.com/blog/what-colors-show-sweat-the-least/), [Ministry of Supply](https://www.ministryofsupply.com/blogs/tested/engineers-notes-moisture-wicking), [Sourcify sandwash guide](https://www.sourcifychina.com/sandwash-fabrics/), [Univ. of Idaho silk care](https://objects.lib.uidaho.edu/uiext/uiext29673.pdf).

#### Lesson 15 — Bisht cloth and zari

The bisht is wool or animal-hair cloth with a gold or silver edge. Today much of the base cloth is imported, and cheap bishts can be polyester. The gold can be real metal or plastic film.

- **Fiber:** UNESCO says wool and camel or goat hair. Abu Dhabi's heritage register says it is sometimes mixed with cotton or silk. Arab News says the costly Hasawi uses camel, llama or goat hair. One UK shop sells a 100% polyester bisht.
- **Weaving and seams:** Kuwaiti weavers used a hand loom set in a pit in the floor. The old cloak is two equal strips sewn across the middle. One wide piece with no seam points to machine weaving. Summer bishts are fine and light; winter ones are thick.
- **Where cloth comes from now:** Kuwait Times says cloth comes from Japan and Britain. Saudipedia says Hasawi bishts use Japanese fabric.
- **Who makes it:** Al-Ahsa families still make them, but most bishts are machine-made now. The Royal Institute of Traditional Arts (Wrth) runs a 23-month Hasawi bisht course in Al-Ahsa.
- **What zari is:** Real zari is silk or cotton yarn wrapped in silver wire, then gold-plated. Imitation zari uses copper wire plated with silver. Since about 1970, some has a plastic core. Lurex is a metallic plastic film.
- **Rule for A'ila Areeqa:** Write the fiber and the zari type on every bisht tag, for example "camel hair; gold-plated silver zari".

#### Lesson 17 — Care in the Gulf

Strong sun is the biggest threat to silk, and hard water harms natural dyes. Saudi rules call for care symbols under SASO-ISO-3758.

- **Silk in strong sun:** Black silk from the Kaaba cover was left outdoors in Makkah for a year. It lost 82% of its strength, but the black dye held well. The study names UV light as the main cause.
- **Cupro:** Dark cupro can bleed in its first washes and fade over time. Dry cleaning is safest. Otherwise hand wash cold, since warm water can shrink it. Iron instead of steam: steam drips can leave dark spots.
- **Wool jackets and canvas:** Dry clean as rarely as you can, about once a season. Brush, steam and air the jacket in between. A badly glued (fused) inner layer can bubble in cleaning. Speculation: a sewn-in canvas avoids this, but no test was found.
- **Natural dyes and hard water:** Hard water weakened natural colors on cotton in one study. One brand says use a neutral soap, cool water and dry in shade. No data was found on Gulf tap-water hardness.
- **Care symbols:** Labels show five symbols in order: tub (wash), triangle (bleach), square (dry), iron, circle (professional clean). In the circle, P and F mean dry-cleaning types; W means professional wet cleaning. One bar under a symbol means gentle; two bars, very gentle.
- **Loro Piana baby camel:** Still no Loro Piana page found. The brand Noah says it bought baby camel cloth from Loro Piana, from Bactrian camels in the Gobi, gathered in spring when they shed.
- **Rule for A'ila Areeqa:** Use ISO 3758 symbols with Arabic text. Add "dry in shade" to every silk and naturally dyed piece.

#### Lesson 18 — Saudi textile rules (SASO)

The SASO textile rule is final and in force. A newer version started on 10 July 2024.

- **First version:** The SASO Board approved it on 5 April 2018. It was published in the Official Gazette on 24 August 2018. SGS gives 23 February 2019 as the start date.
- **New version:** It was adopted on 12 January 2024. Changes had to be in place by 10 July 2024, 180 days later.
- **What a label must show:** Fiber content in % by weight, largest first. Size, weight or dimensions. Country of origin. Care instructions, in words or standard symbols. The supplier's name and commercial registration go on the outer packaging.
- **Language:** The 2018 text says Arabic, or Arabic plus English. The 2024 version (per SGS) requires Arabic; one more language is allowed. Labels must be sewn in or firmly fixed. No Quran verses and no word "Allah" on labels.
- **Sweat fastness:** The final rule requires tests for washing, sweat and rubbing, and names the test standards (for example ISO 105-E04). The final text opened gives no number grade. The "3-4" grade (Lesson 13) appears only in a page about the earlier draft.
- **Certificate:** You need a Certificate of Conformity from a SASO-approved body. Underwear and children's items need a stricter "Type 3" certificate. Speculation: certificates are issued through SABER, the Saudi online system; the PDF does not say.
- **Rule for A'ila Areeqa:** Every piece gets a sewn-in Arabic label showing fiber %, size, origin and care, plus an English line.

#### Lesson 19 — Hard water in Riyadh and Jeddah

The one Riyadh tap study found shows hard water. The one Jeddah home-tank study shows soft water. Both are from 2012.

- **Riyadh:** A 2012 study measured tap water in four districts. Total hardness was about 321–327 mg/L. The paper does not say "as CaCO3".
- **Why Riyadh is hard:** Riyadh mixes desalinated sea water with groundwater. The study says about 65% comes from desalination plants at Jubail.
- **Jeddah:** A 2012 study tested 113 samples from home underground tanks. Average hardness was about 44 mg/L. Groundwater in villages northeast of Jeddah was far harder, at 230–3,659 mg/L.
- **How to read the numbers:** One trade article calls 0–100 ppm soft, 101–250 medium and over 251 hard. By that scale, Riyadh tap is hard and Jeddah tank water is soft. The figures may be out of date.
- **What it does to cloth:** Hard water reacts with detergent and leaves a grey scum, so whites turn grey. A softener seller adds that clothes feel stiff and look dull. Softened water can be more alkaline, which can yellow white fabric.
- **Do Gulf laundries soften water?** No laundry found says so. A Saudi softener seller says laundries use softeners, but that is a sales claim.
- **Rule for A'ila Areeqa:** On Riyadh care cards, advise a laundry that uses softened water for white thobes.

#### Lesson 24 — Long-staple cotton and mercerizing

Longer cotton fibers make finer, stronger, smoother yarn. But only trust the label if you can check it.

- **Staple = fiber length:** Cotton is "extra-long staple" when its fibers are at least 1 3/8 inch long. Supima averages over 1 7/16 inch. Giza 45 is described as 45 mm fibers; normal cotton is about 38 mm. Sea Island cotton grows mainly in Barbados, Antigua and Jamaica, and is very rare.
- **Why longer is better:** Long, fine fibers spin into strong, fine yarn. The cloth is lighter, softer, pills less and holds deeper color. Fewer loose fiber ends stick out, so it is smoother.
- **Mercerizing:** A bath in caustic soda while the cloth is held tight. Each fiber turns rounder, which gives a silk-like shine. It also makes the cloth stronger, take dye better and shrink less.
- **Two-ply:** Two yarns twisted into one thread. The cloth is tighter and heavier than single-ply, and gets softer with time.
- **Fakes are common:** In one DNA test, 83% of products labelled "100% Pima" were partly or fully other cotton. The Cotton Egypt Association says a big share of "Egyptian cotton" is not.
- **How to check:** "Supima" is a trademark; only licensed firms may use it, checked by DNA test. Egyptian cotton has a "Gold Seal" from the Cotton Egypt Association, also backed by DNA tests. Albini (owner of Thomas Mason) has a Giza 45 luxury line, David & John Anderson.
- **Rule for A'ila Areeqa:** Never print "Giza", "Supima" or "Egyptian" on a label without a licence from the mill and a DNA test report for the lot.

Sources: [Textile World](https://www.textileworld.com/textile-world/knitting-apparel/2017/04/5-surprising-facts-about-supima-cotton/), [Fibre2Fashion – Supima](https://www.fibre2fashion.com/market-intelligence/texpro-textile-and-apparel/textile-guide/10538/supima-cotton-fabric), [Kothea – Giza 45](https://www.kothea.com/2014/05/23/high-quality-egyptian-cotton-giza-45/), [Turnbull & Asser](https://turnbullandasser.co.uk/blogs/off-the-cuff/the-holy-grail-of-cotton), [Wikipedia – Mercerisation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mercerisation), [Ledbury – ply](https://www.ledbury.com/blogs/easy-goer/adding-numbers), [Permanent Style – mills](https://www.permanentstyle.com/2017/05/shirting-mills-and-brands-explained.html), [Home Textiles Today – Gold Seal](https://www.hometextilestoday.com/industry-news/welspun-certified-egyptian-cotton-gold-seal).

#### Lesson 25 — Collar and cuff linings

The hidden layer inside a collar decides if it stays crisp or bubbles after washing.

- **Two kinds:** A *fused* lining is glued to the outer cloth. A *floating* lining is stitched only at the edges and moves freely inside.
- **Floating:** Easier to make a thick, stiff collar; softer on the neck. Harder to sew, so most factories can't, and fussier to iron.
- **Fused:** Easier to make, and holds its shape under a jacket. Cheap fusing gave it a bad name. All fusing bubbles in the end; good fusing takes many years.
- **Stiffness comes from weight:** Linings run from about 50 g to 400 g. Weight, not the method, sets stiffness. Some makers mix both.
- **Why bubbles happen:** Too much heat pushes glue into the cloth; too little leaves it on the lining. If the two layers shrink more than 2% apart, the bond can tear. Bubbles may only show after washing.
- **What to ask:** Fused or floating? Lining weight in grams? Lining brand and glue type? A wash test on our exact cloth?
- **Rule for A'ila Areeqa:** Wash-test five sample collars and cuffs before any order. Reject any bubbling.

Speculation: thobe band collars likely follow the same rules; no source covered thobes.

#### Lesson 26 — Viscose, modal, lyocell (Tencel) and cupro

All four are plant fiber dissolved and spun again. They differ most in strength when wet and in how clean the making is.

| Fiber | Made from | Strength when wet | Shrinkage |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Viscose | Wood pulp | Low | Up to 10% |
| Modal | Mostly beech wood | High | Not given |
| Lyocell (Tencel LF) | Wood pulp | 27–29 cN/tex (dry 33–35) | Not given |
| Cupro (Bemberg) | Cotton seed fuzz (linter) | Close to viscose | Not given |

- **Viscose:** Made with a toxic chemical (carbon disulfide). Weak when wet; can shrink up to 10% in a machine wash.
- **Modal:** Made like viscose, but stretched about three times while forming, so it is strong when wet. Lenzing says it is not closed-loop.
- **Lyocell:** Dissolved in a solvent that is over 99.5% recovered and reused (Lenzing). Smooth, breathes well, keeps skin dry.
- **Cupro:** Asahi Kasei reuses its copper and ammonia in a closed loop, and says the fiber breaks down naturally.
- **Why luxury uses them:** Fine rayon fabrics drape like silk.
- **Rule for A'ila Areeqa:** For linings that get washed, ask for modal, lyocell or cupro, not plain viscose. Get the fiber maker's name on the invoice.

#### Lesson 27 — Buttons: shell, horn, corozo or plastic

Real buttons are a quiet sign of quality. Simple touch tests catch most plastic fakes.

- **Mother-of-pearl (MOP):** The inner shiny layer of pearl oysters. It does not melt in washes like resin can, but may crack at a rough dry cleaner.
- **Trocas (trochus):** A different shell. It can look like plastic; MOP shines more and costs more (forum opinion only).
- **Tests:** Touch it to your upper lip: real shell feels cold, plastic does not. Tap it on a tooth: shell clicks sharp, plastic sounds dull. Real MOP shows a rainbow shine when tilted and is polished on both sides.
- **Horn:** Indian water buffalo horn is the best. Ox horn is cheaper; strong yellow on black or dark brown usually means ox.
- **Corozo:** The hard nut of the tagua palm from South America. Cool and heavy, with a slightly uneven grain. Sources disagree on whether it fades.
- **Sewing:** A tailor wraps thread under a sewn-through button to make a "thread shank," so the garment closes neatly. A Savile Row tailor does 4 passes with thick thread, or 6–8 with thin.
- **Rule for A'ila Areeqa:** Use real MOP on shirts and thobes. Do the lip test and tilt test on every new batch.

#### Lesson 29 — Shirt yarn numbers (100/2, 2/120, 140s)

The number is the size of the yarn, not threads per inch. The "/2" means two yarns twisted into one thread.

- **The number:** Proper Cloth says "140s" means 140 hanks of yarn (840 yards each) weigh one pound. Bigger number = finer yarn. Finer yarn makes a smoother, softer, thinner and more costly cloth.
- **How it is written:** "100/2 × 100/2" means 100s two-ply in both directions (warp and weft). Speculation: the "2/120" on some swatches is the same idea written the other way round. Confirm with the mill.
- **Ply:** Two-ply is two yarns twisted together. Sources agree two-ply is usually better: stronger, softer, fewer wrinkles, and a tighter, heavier cloth.
- **Compact yarn:** Proper Cloth says a 100s single-ply "compact" cloth can feel like a 160s two-ply cloth without compact yarn.
- **Watch out:** Some brands use the same words for "threads per square inch." Ledbury and Attire Club use that meaning. So two shops can say "120" and mean different things.
- **For heat:** Ledbury calls 120 "soft, but durable" and says it "breathes easily." It warns that very high numbers can feel uncomfortable and breathe less. Attire Club says light summer shirts often use a lower count than formal shirts.
- **Rule for A'ila Areeqa:** For a summer thobe, start with two-ply 100s to 120s in both directions. Always ask the supplier to write "yarn count (Ne) and ply, for warp and weft," never just "thread count."

Sources: [Proper Cloth – Thread count and ply](https://propercloth.com/reference/thread-count-ply), [Ledbury – Adding numbers](https://www.ledbury.com/blogs/easy-goer/adding-numbers), [Parisian Gentleman – Yarn count](https://www.parisiangentleman.com/blog/super-100s-and-such-understanding-yarn-count-of-shirts-and-suits), [Attire Club – Thread count and ply](https://attireclub.org/2018/07/21/thread-count-and-ply-what-they-mean/).

#### Lesson 30 — The Egyptian Cotton logo licence

Only makers who prove the cotton is 100% Egyptian get to use the logo. The checks are DNA tests on the cloth.

- **Who runs it:** The Cotton Egypt Association (CEA), a non-profit set up in 2005. It manages the Egyptian Cotton logo for its owners, Egypt's Ministry of Trade and Industry and the Alexandria Cotton Exporters' Association.
- **Who gets it:** Manufacturers. Since 2018, only makers found to produce 100% Egyptian cotton goods get accredited. Others lose the right to use the logo.
- **How it is checked:** Bureau Veritas tests DNA from fiber, yarn, woven or knit cloth, or finished clothes. It also audits back to the cotton lot number.
- **Shops that care:** In 2018, UK stores John Lewis and Dunelm said they would make their suppliers meet this standard.
- **Cost:** The only fee found is from 2008: $5,000 to register, then $2,000 a year. That is very old. Ask CEA for today's fee.
- **Rule for A'ila Areeqa:** Never write "Egyptian cotton" on a label unless the mill is CEA-accredited and gives you its paperwork for your exact cloth lot. Speculation: buying from an accredited mill is likely cheaper than getting your own licence. Ask CEA.

Sources: [ICAC – CEA slides](https://icac.org/wp-content/uploads/2024/12/Shuman.pdf), [HFN – Retailers back CEA accreditation (2018)](https://www.hfndigital.com/industry-news/retailers-support-cotton-egypt-associations-accreditation-process), [Home Textiles Today – Egyptian Cotton enforces brand rights (2008)](https://www.hometextilestoday.com/industry-news/egyptian-cotton-enforces-brand-rights/).

#### Lesson 31 — Wet strength: modal, viscose and cupro

Modal is made to stay strong when wet; plain viscose is not. The maker of Bemberg cupro gives no public strength numbers.

- **Modal:** It is made from beech wood pulp. The official fiber rule (BISFA) defines modal by a minimum strength when wet. Fibre2Fashion says it has "higher tenacity and wet modulus than viscose rayon." (Tenacity = strength. Wet modulus = how well it holds its shape when wet.)
- **Why:** In making modal, the fibers are stretched about three times their length. This gives a high wet-to-dry strength ratio.
- **Downsides:** The same article says 100% modal needs ironing and can pill, and costs more than viscose or cotton.
- **A number table:** The article has a wet and dry strength table, but the copy we could read was jumbled. So no numbers are given here.
- **Bemberg cupro:** Asahi Kasei's own sewing guide says Bemberg is cupro made from cotton linter (the short fluff on cotton seeds). It lists no strength numbers.
- **Rule for A'ila Areeqa:** For any wood-based cloth, ask the mill for a wet and dry strength test and a shrink test after one wash. For pieces that get washed often, pick modal or lyocell over plain viscose.

Sources: [Fibre2Fashion – Modal: fibre to fabric](https://www.fibre2fashion.com/industry-article/5169/submitArticleLink?amp=true), [Asahi Kasei – Bemberg sewing guide](https://www.asahi-kasei.co.jp/fibers/en/bemberg/pdf/archives/manual.pdf).

#### Lesson 32 — Pilling: the little balls on cloth

Pilling is tested by rubbing the cloth and grading it from 5 (none) to 1 (very bad). For good shirt cloth, ask for 4 or better.

- **The test:** ISO 12945-2 uses a Martindale machine that rubs the cloth in a pattern. ISO 12945-1 uses a tumbling "pilling box." The cloth is checked at set numbers of rubs, such as 125, 500 and 1,000.
- **The grades:** 5 = no pilling, 4 = slight, 3 = moderate, 2 = bad, 1 = very bad. Half grades like 3.5 are allowed.
- **What shops ask for:** One lab says most retailers want knits at grade 3–4. A software firm's guide says premium woven shirt cloth is often held to 4–5. These are trade habits, not laws.
- **Why some cloth pills more:** Short fibers, low twist, loose weaves, and no anti-pill finish. Polyester and nylon are so strong that pills stay stuck on. Weaker wool pills tend to break off. Washing usually lowers the grade.
- **Rub counts:** Numbers like "20,000 rubs" are for furniture fabric, not clothes.
- **Rule for A'ila Areeqa:** In every order, ask for a pilling grade by ISO 12945-2, tested after one wash: grade 4 for thobe and shirt cloth, 3–4 for knits. Check polyester and nylon blends extra hard.

Sources: [Testex – Martindale FAQs](https://www.testextextile.com/10-faqs-about-martindale-test-and-martindale-abrasion-and-pilling-test-method/), [Begoodtex – ISO 12945](https://begoodtex.com/blog/iso-12945-textile-pilling-and-fuzzing-test-standards/), [Dabedan – Martindale and ISO 12945-2](https://dabedan.com/en/martindale-method-and-en-iso-12945-2-pilling-resistance-testing-for-fabrics/), [iFactory – Pilling test guide](https://ifactoryapp.com/industries/textile-manufacturing/pilling-resistance-testing-martindale-ici-random-tumble).

#### Lesson 33 — Was Gulf pearl shell used for buttons?

No source found says so. But the Gulf did have shells known for mother-of-pearl.

- **Shell names:** Gulf Arabs named oyster types. The small *maḥḥāra* held seed pearls. The big, thick *ṣadaifiya* gave "excellent mother-of-pearl." It was found near Lavan, Hendorabi and Kish, islands on the Iranian side.
- **Old use:** An 1856 British record from Bahrain says the shell, "known by the name of mother-o'-pearl, is used in Asia for various purposes." It does not say which.

#### Lesson 34 — "2/120" or "120/2"? Reading yarn labels

The two numbers are the same idea written two ways. One number is how fine each yarn is. The other is how many yarns are twisted together (the ply).

- **Cotton shirt cloth (English count, Ne):** The fineness comes first, the ply after. A trade article writes "80/2" and "100/2" for two-ply cotton.
- **Wool and metric count (Nm):** The ply often comes first. A yarn shop writes "2/28,000" and says you divide: 28,000 ÷ 2 = 14,000, the real thickness of the plied yarn.
- **So:** "2/120" and "120/2" most likely both mean two yarns of size 120, twisted together. Speculation: no source today states this as a rule, so always ask the mill.
- **2x2:** A "2x2" shirt has two-ply yarn both ways (length and width). Watch out: some makers use two-ply one way and single the other, and still call it "two-ply."
- **Why two-ply:** Twisting two yarns makes the thread stronger. Shirt writers say it gives a softer, stronger shirt that creases less. Higher numbers mean finer yarn, but also less tough.
- **Rule for A'ila Areeqa:** On every swatch, ask the mill to write it out in words: "count \_\_\_ (Ne or Nm), \_\_\_ ply, warp and weft." Only accept "2x2" if both warp and weft are two-ply.

#### Lesson 35 — Compact yarn

Compact spinning is a newer way to spin cotton (from 1999). It squeezes the loose fibers tight before twisting. The result is a smoother, stronger yarn with fewer hairs sticking out.

- **Numbers from a 2007 trade article:** 20–30% less hairiness on one test, 10–18% stronger, 20–35% fewer weak spots. Two-ply compact yarn (sold as "Wondertwist") was about 12–17% stronger than normal two-ply.
- **Makers:** Rieter (brand COM4) and Suessen (brand EliTe) are the leaders.
- **In the cloth (Suessen figures, 2008):** Less shrinkage (3–4% vs 5–6%), about 30% less wear, softer hand, more shine. The shine after mercerizing lasts through many washes. Better pilling resistance.
- **Price:** The 2007 article says compact yarn can cost the mill less, because some steps are skipped. So it should not cost much more.
- **Careful:** These numbers come from machine makers and trade press, not from independent tests.
- **Not found:** Whether thobe cloth labels say "compact." The Day 6 question stays open.
- **Rule for A'ila Areeqa:** For thobe and shirt cotton, ask: "Is this compact-spun?" Prefer compact two-ply for white cloth, where loose hairs make the surface look dull and pill.

Sources: [Fibre2Fashion – compact spinning (2007)](https://www.fibre2fashion.com/industry-article/2905/compact-spinning-innovation-of-21st-century), [Fibre2Fashion – compact yarn in knitting (2008, Suessen)](https://www.fibre2fashion.com/industry-article/3118/structural-advantages-of-compact-yarn-in-knitting), [Fabricateurialist – COM4 by Rieter (2025)](https://fabricateurialist.substack.com/p/com4-by-rieter).

#### Lesson 36 — Pre-shrinking (sanforizing)

Cotton cloth that is not treated can shrink 6–10% in the first wash. Sanforizing shrinks it at the mill first, so the garment keeps its size.

- **How it works:** The damp cloth is pressed against a thick rubber belt that is stretched, then let go. As the belt shrinks back, it pushes the cloth tighter. Width shrinkage is handled on a separate frame (tentering).
- **The result:** Under 1% shrinkage left in the cloth.
- **History:** Named after Sanford Lockwood Cluett, who patented it in 1930.
- **The trademark:** "Sanforized" is a registered mark of Cluett, Peabody and Co. Cloth with the mark is tested to shrink no more than 1% either way. "Sanforized-Plus" is a related mark; ask what it covers.
- **Rule for A'ila Areeqa:** For every cotton or linen order, ask for residual shrinkage after washing, in writing, both ways. Aim for 1% or less for thobes and shirts. A thobe that shrinks 3% loses about 4 cm in length.

Sources: [Heddels – Sanforization](https://www.heddels.com/?p=1547), [Kothea – Sanforisation explained](https://www.kothea.com/tag/shirt/), [iFactory – sanforizing guide (2026)](https://ifactoryapp.com/industries/textile-manufacturing/sanforizing-compressive-shrinkage-control-standards), [Innovation in Textiles – Monforts sanforizer (2013)](https://www.innovationintextiles.com/monforts-sanforisor-ensures-precise-control-of-compressive-shrinkage-for-pincroft).

#### Lesson 37 — Fresco: high-twist wool for heat

Fresco is a wool cloth made to let air through. It was patented in 1907 by Martin Sons & Co. in England. Today Hardy Minnis makes it, inside the Huddersfield Fine Worsteds group. "Fresco" is a registered trademark.

- **How it is made:** Merino wool yarn is twisted very hard, then several of these yarns are twisted together. They are woven in a simple, open plain weave.
- **Why it works:** The open weave lets wind pass, but the cloth is not see-through. The hard twist makes it spring back, so it barely creases.
- **Weights:** About 250–310 g per meter for the lighter kinds, up to 435–465 g for three-ply. Shops sell summer suits at about 280 g.
- **The feel:** A dry, rough hand. Makers sell this as part of its character.
- **Rule for A'ila Areeqa:** For a winter-evening bisht-style coat or tailored trousers worn in Gulf heat, test a high-twist wool near 250–280 g before choosing polyester. Ask the mill if it is real Fresco or a "high-twist" copy, and feel it on bare skin first.

Sources: [Husbands Paris – The fresco (2026)](https://husbands-paris.com/en/blogs/journal/the-fresco), [Hast – Le Fresco (2025)](https://www.hastparis.com/en/blogs/journal/le-fresco%c2%ae-l-art-et-la-matiere), [Trunk Clothiers – What is fresco](https://trunkclothiers.com/blogs/journal/fabric-stories-what-is-fresco), [Articles of Style – fresco suit](https://articlesofstyle.com/blogs/news/fresco-suit-for-hot-weather).

#### Lesson 38 — How Prada made nylon a luxury fiber

Prada's 1984 nylon backpack was "the first step of nylon into luxury." The cloth is a nylon gabardine, "hard-wearing yet lustrous," woven on silk looms.

- **Re-Nylon:** Launched June 2019 with six bags. It uses Econyl, made by Aquafil from waste such as fishing nets and ocean plastic.
- **The promise:** Prada aimed to replace all its new (virgin) nylon with Econyl by the end of 2021. Econyl is said to cut global-warming impact by up to 90% vs new nylon, and to be recyclable again and again without losing quality. These are brand claims.
- **Into clothes:** By October 2020, Re-Nylon covered coats, dresses, padded jackets, trousers and t-shirts. Prada also has a very fine nylon, *Piuma*, "with the feel of silk."
- **The lesson:** A synthetic can be luxury when the weave is fine, the shine is low and controlled, and there is a clear story.
- **Rule for A'ila Areeqa:** If you use nylon, use it where it does a job (rain shell, travel bag, lining of a winter coat). Choose a fine, low-shine weave. Ask for regenerated nylon with a certificate, and do not claim "sustainable" without proof.

#### Lesson 41 — Yarn-dyed, piece-dyed, garment-dyed

When the color goes in changes how long it lasts.

- **Yarn-dyed:** The yarn is colored first, then woven. Used for stripes, checks and denim.
- **Piece-dyed:** The cloth is woven plain, then dyed whole. Most solid colors are made this way.
- **Which lasts:** Piece-dyed color is less fast than yarn-dyed. Yarn-dyed cloth costs more.
- **Garment-dyed:** The sewn garment is dyed. One maker says its garment-dyed shirts do not shrink in the wash, while piece-dyed shirts shrink a little the first time.
- **Careful:** Stripes can also be printed, to look yarn-dyed.
- **Rule for A'ila Areeqa:** Any stripe or check must be yarn-dyed, never printed. For solid colors, ask for the sweat and rubbing grades (Lesson 13), since piece-dyed color is weaker.

Sources: [James Dunlop Textiles](https://faq.jamesdunloptextiles.com/knowledge/yarn-dye-vs-piece-dye.-what-is-the-difference), [Online Clothing Study](https://onlineclothingstudy.com/2015/12/what-is-difference-between-piece-dyed.html), [Goodwear](https://goodwear.com/pages/garment-dyed-vs-piece-dyed).

#### Lesson 42 — The four main knits

Knits are made of loops, not crossed threads.

| Knit | Looks | Stretch | Edges | Used for |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Single jersey | Smooth face, loops on back | Width about 2× length | Curls when cut | T-shirts |
| Rib | Same both sides | Width about 2× single jersey | Does not curl | Cuffs, collars, waistbands |
| Interlock | Same both sides, smooth | Stable | Does not curl | Fine polos, baby clothes |
| Piqué | Tiny mesh holes | — | — | Polos, hot weather |

- **Weight and cost:** Interlock is two jerseys knit together. It uses more yarn, so it is heavier and costs more.
- **Jersey weight:** Often 120–220 g/m² (Wikipedia, no source given).
- **Luxury note:** Interlock in pima cotton is called an elegant alternative to piqué for polos.
- **Rule for A'ila Areeqa:** For a quiet-luxury polo, use interlock for the smooth look. Use piqué when air matters more. Ask for rib at the collar and cuffs.

Sources: [CottonWorks – Single and double knits](https://cottonworks.com/learning-hub/knitting/single-and-double-knits/), [Wikipedia – Jersey](<https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jersey_(fabric)>), [Minerva Patterns – Knit types](https://minervapatterns.com/blog/knit-fabric-types-jersey-rib-interlock-and-ponte).

#### Lesson 43 — Voile, lawn and batiste

All three are thin plain-weave cottons. Shops often mix up the names.

- **Voile:** French for "veil." Made from tightly twisted yarns. The most see-through of the three. Soft and drapey.
- **Lawn:** Fine combed cotton with a high thread count. Crisper than voile. Named after Laon, a French town known for linen. Liberty's Tana Lawn is 240 threads.
- **Batiste:** Named after a 13th-century weaver, Jean Baptiste. Soft. Recommended for shirts. Very close to lawn.
- **Muslin:** A looser, open weave.
- **Sewing them (Threads magazine):** Use French seams. Fine needle (65/9 or 70/10), 2.5 mm stitch, fine cotton thread. Wash the cloth before cutting.
- **Not found:** Weights in g/m² for each.
- **Rule for A'ila Areeqa:** For summer under-layers and see-through women's pieces, ask for lawn or batiste and French seams only. Ask the supplier for the weight, since names alone mean little.

Sources: [The Dreamstress](https://thedreamstress.com/2020/05/voile-lawn-muslin-whats-the-difference-the-short-answer/), [Threads magazine](https://www.threadsmagazine.com/2023/01/18/finely-woven-batiste-lawn-and-voile), [Wikipedia – Lawn cloth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lawn_cloth).
